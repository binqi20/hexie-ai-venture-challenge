# Case 03: HearthDose
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

## 1. Venture Brief

An older person can have the right medicines at home and still find the weekly routine difficult. Different packaging, changed prescriptions and uncertainty about whether a dose was taken can make family support complicated. Team 03 proposes HearthDose for families supporting an older relative with an established medication schedule. The person taking the medicine remains the service user, even when a relative pays.

HearthDose combines pharmacist-prepared weekly blister packs with a short weekly telephone check-in. A qualified pharmacy partner would decide whether the prescribed medicines can be packed safely and would handle preparation and checking. Our role would cover subscriptions, agreed contact arrangements and referrals back to the pharmacist. We would never select medicines or recommend changes to doses.

Our initial idea was a reminder application. In a fictional desk exercise, we wrote four household situations and walked through missed reminders, prescription changes and a relative living elsewhere. Two situations still required a person to resolve confusion. This is a synthetic reasoning exercise, not evidence from patients.

Families would pay a monthly subscription. Community health centres are a possible later purchaser, although their approval process is unknown. Our working price assumption is RMB 180 per month, excluding the medicines themselves. We estimate a direct service cost of RMB 132, leaving RMB 48 before administration and overheads. Families would pay more than for a reminder-only product because pharmacist preparation and human contact consume time.

|---|---|---|
| Packaging and a weekly call address a problem families value | Untested outside our fictional household exercise | Discuss current routines with consenting service users and relatives |
| A pharmacy partner can prepare suitable packs safely | Untested | Ask a qualified partner to assess scope, workflow and responsibility |

## 3. Stakeholder Response Log

### 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received

These are fictional stakeholder challenges within this synthetic submission. They are not statements from actual consultees.

**Investor:** Your proposed margin is narrow. If calls take longer than expected, the service could lose money on the people who need it most. Start with a fixed call allowance and consider a higher-priced support tier. Explain which costs the subscription covers before promising discounted places or centre contracts.

**Customer:** As the person using the medicines, I would want a familiar caller who can explain what happens when I am unsure. A strict time allowance could discourage me from asking. My family may pay, but I do not want every conversation or missed call reported to them. Let me agree what they receive.

**Supplier/Operations Partner:** As a pharmacy operator, I would need an up-to-date prescription and a clear process for changes. Some medicines may be unsuitable for your pack format. Repacking also takes checking time and materials.

**Regulator:** I cannot confirm the permissions your proposed arrangement would require. You should obtain appropriate professional guidance on medicine handling, information protection and the division of responsibilities. A telephone assistant should not interpret symptoms or advise a person to alter medication. Show where a concern goes and how the user understands the service limits.

**Community/Ethics Stakeholder:** The users with the least money may also have limited family support. A family subscription could exclude them. Telephone contact helps people who do not use applications, but it still excludes some people with hearing or communication difficulties.

The immediate priority is the service user's safety and consent. The pharmacy comes next as an essential delivery dependency. We still need paying households, but their preferences cannot override these conditions. The community request changed our price discussion: supported places have to appear in the operating budget instead of being promised from unspecified future profits.

## 4. Decision Memo

Our judgement is **pause** before any delivery of medicines, while continuing prospective demand and partner discussions. We recommend a limited service only after a qualified pharmacy partner accepts the arrangement and professional review clarifies responsibilities. We have a plausible problem and a service outline, but no patient evidence or delivery approval.

The most useful evidence currently is our synthetic household walkthrough. A reminder can address forgetting, but it cannot resolve uncertainty following a prescription change. That supports keeping a pharmacist referral route. It does not tell us how common the problem is or whether families will subscribe. We therefore narrowed the offer to people with an established routine whom the pharmacy considers suitable, rather than promising support for every chronic condition.

Our assumed monthly direct cost is RMB 72 for preparation and checking, RMB 28 for calls, RMB 20 for packaging and collection coordination, and RMB 12 for expected rework. The total is RMB 132. At an assumed price of RMB 180, a standard subscription contributes RMB 48 before shared costs. These are planning estimates, not quotations. If preparation costs increase by RMB 20 and longer calls add RMB 15, that contribution falls to RMB 13. We have not established the fixed-cost requirement or break-even subscriber number.

The main conflict is between predictable costs and sufficient time for a vulnerable user's questions. We have rejected a call that ends automatically when an allowance expires. The caller would handle agreed practical questions and refer clinical concerns. Repeated long calls would trigger a review of suitability with the user and pharmacy. This creates a risk that the service cannot support some high-need users; we need a fair referral arrangement before accepting them.

For access, our illustrative budget reserves one supported place for every nine standard subscriptions. At an assumed supported price of RMB 90, that place costs RMB 42 more than it contributes. Nine standard contributions total RMB 432, so RMB 390 remains across the ten places before overheads. The venture initially bears that reduction. We cannot present this as broad affordability, and it only works if paying subscriptions exist.

Next we would seek a partner workflow review and prospective interviews with users and family purchasers. We would ask about existing support, packaging preferences and price objections separately. Remaining uncertainty concerns willingness to pay, medicine suitability, call duration and the cost of reaching households.

### 5.3 Anticipated harms and response

A relative could use the service to monitor someone who does not want that involvement. We propose obtaining the user's agreement to contact and routine disclosure separately. The practical difficulty is that some users may need assistance to understand the arrangement. We have not settled how that assessment should work and would seek appropriate guidance before enrolment.

A caller might receive a worrying description of symptoms and feel pressure to help. The script would explain that the service cannot assess symptoms and would use a professionally reviewed escalation route. We have not yet designed that route adequately, which is another reason to pause delivery. Missed calls would not automatically be treated as missed doses.

Weekly packaging creates waste and could create false confidence if a prescription changes. The pharmacy would need to specify what happens to superseded packs and how users identify the current one.

Supported places may create disappointment when demand exceeds the allocation. We would publish the limited availability before collecting applications and avoid using discounts as a marketing promise for everyone. Additional subsidy would need an identified payer.

### 5.4 Alignment thesis

Reliable preparation and respectful contact may help households and give them a reason to retain the service. However, pharmacist time, longer conversations and supported places consume resources. Our standard price pays for the basic offer; the venture bears the proposed reduction in contribution for supported places. Wider access would require a purchaser or explicit funding. We will not describe safety checks as optional extras, and we will not claim affordability from a discount that reaches only a small group.

## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt

**Opportunity Recognition question: What makes your problem more specific than forgetting medicine?**

**Team 03:** We are concerned with an older person's weekly routine when a relative helps from elsewhere. Packaging may make that routine visible, while a call gives somewhere to raise practical uncertainty. Our desk exercise helped us distinguish forgetting from confusion. We still need users to tell us whether that distinction reflects their experience.

**Feasibility Judgement question: Why pause when your calculation shows a positive contribution?**

**Team 03:** A positive contribution is only one condition. The pharmacy must confirm what it can safely prepare and at what cost. Our calculation excludes shared overheads, and a modest increase in checking and call time leaves little contribution. We would continue low-cost discovery work during the pause. We would not accept a paid medicine service before the delivery and responsibility questions are resolved.

**Stakeholder Reasoning question: How did you decide between the investor's time limit and the user's request?**

**Team 03:** We accepted the need to measure call time. We rejected automatically ending clarification because the caller might discover an issue near the time limit. A clinical concern goes to the agreed professional route. Persistent practical needs require a suitability review. We recognise the investor could still reject this model if enough households need more time than our price can support.

**Ethical Positioning question: Who pays for your access commitment?**

**Team 03:** Initially the venture receives less contribution. Our supported subscription is below estimated direct cost, so standard subscriptions have to cover that shortfall. This is limited access, and we should be clear about it. If the subsidy prevents us covering overheads, we would seek an explicit purchaser rather than quietly removing safety work or charging users unexpected fees.

**Venture Communication question: What should a family understand after hearing your offer?**

**Team 03:** It is a proposed practical support service with pharmacist-prepared packs and a weekly call. It does not replace clinical advice or guarantee that medicines are taken. Our next decision depends on partner review and household evidence, so today we are asking for feedback on that limited proposal.
