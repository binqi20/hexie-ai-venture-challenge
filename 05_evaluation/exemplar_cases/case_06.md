# Case 06: Rainward Genetics
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

## 1. Venture Brief

Rainward Genetics proposes to license gene-edited, drought-tolerant rice seed to farmers in northern Jiangsu. Team 06 sees an opportunity to connect agricultural biotechnology with demand for climate resilience. Our proposition is a premium seed licence supported by accessible guidance, positioning farmers to participate in a more sustainable food system.

The target customer is a farmer who wants greater confidence in the harvest and is open to innovation. Cooperatives would provide a route to groups of customers. Our starting assumption is that concern about water availability makes drought tolerance attractive across this market. We have not selected a particular variety or defined a drought condition.

The solution combines a licensed trait, seed supply through a multiplication partner and a customer support package. Our role would be to connect scientific capability with agricultural demand and coordinate the commercial relationship. A trait owner and multiplication partner have yet to be identified.

Revenue would come from an annual licence bundled into the seed purchase. Our illustrative team assumptions are 100 participating farms and an average annual licence payment of RMB 600, giving RMB 60,000 in licence revenue. This is an aspiration-based scenario, not observed demand. All discussions and figures in this submission are fictional planning material; no field trial, sale or participant activity has occurred.

## 3. Stakeholder Response Log

### 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received

**Investor:** You describe a scalable licence business, but I cannot see what you control. Who owns the trait, what rights would you acquire and why would the owner need you? I would want a protected commercial position and recurring customer payments before treating the revenue scenario as investable. General interest in resilience does not establish demand for this seed.

**Customer:** I would compare the offer with the seed I already use. What happens to yield when the season is wet rather than dry? An annual licence adds another commitment, and I want to know whether I can save seed or leave the arrangement. Please give me a choice I can assess, with terms I understand, before asking for recurring payments.

**Supplier/Operations Partner:** As a prospective seed multiplication partner, I would need a defined variety, lawful access to the material and specifications for identity and quality. Multiplication takes planning and capacity. Your launch ambitions cannot determine when suitable seed becomes available. Who would pay for unsuccessful batches and handle a quality complaint?

**Regulator:** I cannot infer the applicable route from the phrase gene-edited seed. The particular material, intended activity and jurisdiction would need qualified review. Please seek appropriate guidance before assuming that trials, multiplication or commercial distribution can proceed. A sustainability objective does not establish authorisation, and this panel statement is not legal advice.

**Community/Ethics Stakeholder:** A farmer's purchase could affect neighbouring growers and people who do not sign your licence. How would you consider movement of genetic material, dependence on a single seed source and unequal access to alternatives? A brochure explaining your intention does not establish that those affected have meaningful influence over the decision.

## 4. Decision Memo

**Recommendation: proceed.** Rainward Genetics offers a timely proposition at the intersection of resilient agriculture, biotechnology and recurring service revenue. The opportunity is to translate scientific possibility into a recognisable customer promise: a seed choice aligned with greater confidence under water stress.

Our key evidence is the consistency of the concept across the AI-assisted opportunity review, business model and stakeholder preparation. Each exercise returned to resilience as a valuable customer benefit. The illustrative revenue scenario shows how a customer base could become a repeatable licence stream.

We recommend moving towards launch preparation. The proposed licensing structure limits our need to establish a full research operation. Scientific partners would provide the trait and operating partners would support multiplication. We expect the technical requirements to become manageable through the selection of credible partners.

The principal stakeholder conflict concerns the investor's preference for recurring payments and the farmer's preference for flexibility. Our resolution is to retain annual licensing while presenting it as continuing access to innovation and support. Clear communication should help customers appreciate the value of renewal.

The ethical trade-off concerns the pace of innovation and public confidence. Waiting for every concern to be resolved could delay access to a potentially useful product. We therefore favour responsible progress, combining a positive sustainability narrative with openness to feedback. The regulator's points will be reflected in the roadmap, and community questions will inform educational materials.

The final recommendation is to prepare a partner presentation, a farmer-facing introduction and a commercial launch outline. The next milestone is a persuasive proposition that brings the parties together. Remaining uncertainty includes partner availability, customer response and the final operating timetable. We consider these normal features of an early venture and expect them to narrow as market engagement improves. We have not defined a condition that would require abandoning the concept, because the resilience opportunity can be expressed through different varieties and partnership arrangements.

### 5.3 Anticipated harms and response

The regulator raised uncertainty about the route to trials and distribution. Our response is to include a regulatory-engagement workstream within the launch outline. The intended partner presentation will explain our commitment to appropriate requirements and invite prospective partners to indicate how they would support the process. We have not assigned a budget or named a person to lead this workstream.

The community representative asked about consequences beyond the purchasing farmer. We would respond with an accessible explanation of gene editing and our commitment to agricultural sustainability. Public understanding is important when a technology feels unfamiliar. A frequently asked questions page and an open feedback channel would provide a visible route for concerns.

Annual licensing could also create affordability concerns. We would explain that the licence includes ongoing access to innovation and that a premium product needs a sustainable commercial model. We expect cooperative relationships to make the offer easier to understand. Customers who prefer another seed would retain the ability to consider alternatives.

These responses would be brought together under our responsible-innovation message. We have not developed a separate process for neighbouring growers who do not become customers. We anticipate that clear public information and constructive partner relationships would provide an appropriate foundation for engagement as the venture grows.

### 5.4 Alignment thesis

Rainward Genetics links commercial growth with the aspiration of more resilient agriculture. We expect farmer confidence to encourage adoption, while recurring licences support continued innovation and customer support. The intended water-related benefit gives the venture a clear sustainability position. Responsible partners and accessible communication would help maintain trust as the business expands. We see commercial and social value moving together through a shared commitment to agricultural progress, with implementation detail refined through future partnerships.

## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt

**Question 1, Opportunity Recognition:** What observation shows that these farmers need your particular seed?

**Team 06:** Our starting point is the relevance of resilience to agriculture. Farmers operate in an environment where confidence matters, and a drought-tolerant proposition gives them an additional way to think about the future. The next step is to communicate this opportunity effectively and invite farmers to become part of the conversation. We believe a clear proposition will help demand become visible.

**Question 2, Feasibility Judgement:** What would you actually license, and what must be established before seed can be sold?

**Team 06:** We would license the opportunity to access a drought-tolerant seed offer through a coordinated partnership. The precise scientific package would emerge from discussions with the right technical organisation. Our strength is bringing the commercial proposition together and maintaining a customer focus. Regulatory engagement is included in our roadmap, so we see it as part of implementation. At this stage, the priority is demonstrating enough commercial potential to attract those partners.

**Question 3, Stakeholder Reasoning:** You have postponed the multiplication partner's questions. Why should that partner support your timetable?

**Team 06:** A partner would gain access to a venture with a clear customer narrative and a commitment to growth. Our timetable expresses ambition and gives the parties something concrete to work towards. We would remain collaborative as details emerge. The investor and customer conversations are important because they generate the confidence that makes operational discussions worthwhile. With a shared vision, we expect the partnership to find a constructive way forward.

**Question 4, Ethical Positioning:** Does assigning scientific work to a partner reduce potential effects on neighbouring growers?

**Team 06:** Partner selection is central to our responsible-innovation approach. We would look for an organisation that understands the technology and can explain it clearly. Our own role is to build trust and ensure that sustainability remains visible in the offer. A public information page would help people understand the purpose of the venture, and we would welcome questions.

**Question 5, Venture Communication:** Give one reason to stop, rather than continue promoting this proposal.

**Team 06:** The proposal has flexibility because it can evolve with customer needs and partner capabilities. If one version does not attract sufficient interest, we would refine the message or explore another partnership. Our central commitment is to agricultural resilience, which gives us room to adapt while preserving the venture's purpose. We would therefore treat early obstacles as information for the next stage and keep building towards a proposition that stakeholders can support.
