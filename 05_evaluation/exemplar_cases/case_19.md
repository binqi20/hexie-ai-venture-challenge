# Case 19: StallSignal
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

## 1. Venture Brief

Team 19 proposes StallSignal, pesticide-residue test strips with a reusable display card for vegetable stalls. We think shoppers would feel more comfortable buying vegetables if they could see when a sample had been checked. Stall owners would buy strip packs and put the latest result beside the produce. The card would show the vegetable, sampling time, and a simple explanation of the strip reading.

Our starting idea was a testing service visiting the whole market. We changed to a kit because visits would require people to travel and stall owners could probably do the test themselves after training. This would also make the service available when produce arrives. We have no actual stall observations or customer interviews. The problem comes from our discussion of what shoppers might worry about when the supply history is difficult to see.

The immediate customer is a stall owner who wants regular shoppers to trust the stall. The shopper benefits from information but would not buy a strip directly. We would sell refill packs and include the card with the starter pack. A market organiser could buy packs for smaller stalls. We would begin with leafy vegetables because this seems a practical category, although suitability for a particular strip still needs checking.

Our team planning assumption is a selling price of RMB 4 per strip and a supply cost of RMB 2. We assume a stall uses one strip each trading day. The difference would help pay for cards, explanations, replacement tests, and support. We have not obtained a quotation. We would like to keep the price affordable even if those extras cost more than expected.

|---|---|---|
| Visible testing makes shoppers more willing to buy | Untested | Show a sample card and ask whether it changes their choice |
| Stall owners can interpret strips during normal work | Untested | Ask owners to follow draft instructions using demonstration materials |
| Suitable strips fit our assumed supply price | Untested | Request specifications and quotations from possible suppliers |

## 3. Stakeholder Response Log

### 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received

**Investor:** Your assumed difference between purchase and selling price looks narrow once advice and replacement strips are included. Sell through market organisers and use the same pack everywhere, with remote instructions. Avoid providing a person at each market. I would want recurring refill orders and a clear way to prevent support costs rising faster than sales.

**Customer:** As a stall owner, I want a low price and a result customers understand immediately. I cannot spend a long time explaining chemistry while serving people. Include unlimited replacements when a reading is unclear. A straightforward colour card would help, but I would also want somebody available at the stall when shoppers question a result.

**Supplier/Operations Partner:** You are assuming that the strip, vegetable sample, and preparation method work together. Select a limited product range and insist on training before users display results. Sample handling and storage instructions need attention. Replacements should cover defective materials, while wasted strips from incorrect preparation should normally be charged for.

**Regulator:** You may need to establish which product requirements and public claims apply before offering this service. I cannot confirm compliance or provide legal advice. A strip may address a limited substance or group of substances, so the display should avoid implying comprehensive food safety. Consider withholding public results until the sampling method and communication have been reviewed by an appropriate specialist.

**Community/Ethics Stakeholder:** Small stalls should have access even if they cannot pay for training or waste several strips while learning. Offer free replacement tests and in-person assistance. Shoppers who cannot read detailed instructions also need understandable information. A large green symbol could be mistaken for an assurance about all the vegetables, especially when batches change during the day.

We added these changes to the pack description. Training will be compulsory but short. Remote help and visits will be available. The replacement policy should encourage careful use while remaining generous.

## 4. Decision Memo

Our judgement is **proceed** with preparing a small launch. StallSignal gives stall owners something visible to say about the care they take. Before the panel, we concentrated on the strip. We now think the card and the explanation are also important parts of the offer. People need to see the information at the place where they decide what to buy.

The main evidence available is our pack sketch, our assumed costs, and the issues raised in the fictional panel. We have no laboratory comparison or trial sales. The sketch suggests that the service is simple enough to explain, and the panel gives us a list of improvements. We think the need for reassurance makes it worth continuing, although a customer might like the idea and still refuse to pay.

The biggest conflict is between low operating costs and the support needed by stall owners. We resolved this in the proposal by keeping remote instructions as the normal route and adding visits when they are needed. The investor therefore gets a standard pack while customers can still request personal help. Market organisers could help coordinate this. We assume visits would be occasional because owners should become familiar with the process. We have not estimated how many requests might arrive together.

There is also a cost to giving free repeats. Our first plan is for Team 19 to absorb this from the amount left after buying the strips. Smaller stalls should pay the same low price as other stalls. If demand for repeats becomes too high, the organiser could contribute, or the refill price could increase slightly. We cannot yet say who would agree to that increase. Charging for avoidable preparation mistakes could protect the margin, although we also want owners to repeat a test whenever they are uncertain.

For technical feasibility, a supplier would need to confirm a suitable strip and help with instructions. For public claims, we would request a review before letting stalls display results. We still recommend preparing the starter pack and approaching organisers now so interest is not lost while we arrange this advice. A leaflet can explain that a sample result has limits.

The remaining uncertainties are willingness to pay, correct use, and the cost of supporting stalls. We would look at these during the first trial. If the cost seems too high, we would try a larger order to reduce the supply price. Our recommendation is to keep the inclusive offer while finding practical ways to deliver it.

## 5. Venture Value Map

### 5.1 Industry mapping

We use Agricultural Products (FB-AG) as the closest industry because the service concerns vegetables being offered for sale. We are proposing a testing product rather than growing crops, so the fit is approximate. Food Safety seems most relevant to the buyer-facing promise. Water Management and Workforce Health & Safety also matter when owners prepare samples and dispose of used materials. Ingredient Sourcing helps us think about the produce information behind a card.

The industry and topic labels follow the SASB Agricultural Products industry standard (2018). We selected them because they connect with daily stall work. This is a planning exercise using our own assumptions, with no operating evidence. Readiness below describes the plans we have written, rather than proof that a safeguard works.

The Food Safety gap needs specialist advice. Other intentions still need writing into instructions. We have not allocated separate topic costs because a training session could cover several issues.

### 5.3 Anticipated harms and response

The regulator raised the risk that a result looks more comprehensive than it is. We would print “sample tested” on the card and include a short explanation underneath. A larger information sheet could describe what the strip does. We still prefer a simple coloured indicator because shoppers may not stop to read much. We would ask the specialist to help make those parts work together.

The community stakeholder raised unequal access. Free repeats and help at the stall should make participation easier. Team 19 would initially carry the extra cost, which reduces the money available for expansion. We would also ask organisers whether they could support owners who need more help. We think shared responsibility is sensible, although nobody has agreed to provide that support.

An unclear result could embarrass the stall owner. We have not decided whether the old card should stay up during a repeat.

### 5.4 Alignment thesis

More understandable testing information may help owners gain trust, but affordable participation means we must spend on training and free repeats. Our current intention is to pay these costs from refill income and seek organiser support where needed. The service will therefore earn less per pack when owners need extra help. We think that is acceptable if it encourages regular use. We still need to find out whether the same price can cover both confident users and stalls requiring repeated assistance.

## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt

**Opportunity Recognition: What makes this an opportunity beyond a general concern about food?**

**Team 19:** The information would be right beside the vegetables, so it is useful when buying. Our first idea required a visiting service, whereas the kit puts testing in the owner's hands. We cannot yet show that owners want it. We think shoppers wanting reassurance is a reasonable starting point, and owners could use the card to explain their care. Asking both groups would help us improve the offer.

**Feasibility Judgement: What happens if free repeats remove the assumed margin?**

**Team 19:** We would talk to the supplier about a lower price and encourage organisers to buy larger packs. Owners should also need fewer repeats once they learn. If that does not work, there may need to be a small price increase. We have not calculated the exact point for changing the price because the strip price itself is still an assumption. We would watch the cost closely during the trial.

**Stakeholder Reasoning: How can you offer local assistance and satisfy the request for remote support only?**

**Team 19:** Remote support would come first, and a visit would be the extra option. That gives each side something useful. We appreciate that the investor wanted no visits, but we hope showing how they help customers will make the investor comfortable. We have not selected who would visit. The organiser might help with this because they are already at the market.

**Ethical Positioning: Could a shopper understand the card as a promise that every vegetable is safe?**

**Team 19:** Yes, which is why we changed the wording to refer to a sample. The colour will still attract attention, but the explanation should make the meaning clearer. We would not want to mislead people. If shoppers misunderstand it, we can make the text larger or ask owners to explain. We need advice about exactly what a particular strip result allows us to say.

**Venture Communication: Give the central proposal and its most important unresolved issue.**

**Team 19:** StallSignal sells test strips and a display card so vegetable sellers can show recent sample testing. The unresolved issue is making the whole pack affordable with enough support. Product suitability matters as well, and we need to check that before use. We believe the idea is worth taking forward because the display is easy to understand and the supplies can be replaced regularly.
