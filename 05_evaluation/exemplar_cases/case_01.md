# Case 01: WeftSignal
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

## 1. Venture Brief

Team 01 proposes a camera service for small weaving mills in Wujiang. The buyer is the production manager responsible for repeated fabric runs. A camera beside a loom identifies a possible recurring defect; the operator checks the alert and decides whether to intervene. Processing stays on the device. We would charge a monthly fee per monitored loom, including installation support and replacement equipment.

Our starting idea was a general visual inspection product. We narrowed it after comparing fictional production records. All working records, observations and calculations in this submission are SYNTHETIC desk evidence, not contact with real mills. In our constructed examples, a defect found at final inspection can affect a longer length of cloth than one noticed during weaving. However, a visible mark is not automatically commercially important. An operator may accept it for one order and reject it for another.

This makes the initial opportunity narrower: repeated orders with an agreed defect definition and a recoverable cost of late detection. A bounded local-processing prototype now seems achievable within our assumed resources. We will compare it against better manual checking before asking a mill to pay. Customer purchase timing remains untested.

Our working monthly price is an assumption of RMB 700 per loom. Estimated recurring cost is RMB 420, leaving RMB 280 before central overhead. No customer has accepted this price.

| Assumption | Tested/Untested | How we would test it |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier alerts prevent avoidable scrap | Untested in production | Compare inspected cloth and accepted interventions against the mill's existing routine |
| Repeated fabric patterns permit useful detection | Tested only on constructed images | Hold back complete rolls and repeat under changing light |
| A manager would pay our assumed price | Untested | Offer a cancellable paid trial after showing measured savings |
| Installation avoids sensitive worker images | Untested on site | Have operators inspect the camera view before any recording |
| Support fits our cost allowance | Untested | Log installation and service time, including failed visits |

## 2. AI-Use Declaration: Stage 1

We used a general-purpose language model for market-search terms, alternative business models, stakeholder rehearsal and first drafts. A coding assistant generated image transformations and a spreadsheet formula sketch. These outputs helped us decide what to examine; they supplied no external market evidence.

We checked the cost formulas by calculating each row separately. We inspected every generated image and kept transformations of the same base image together when dividing the data. Otherwise, near-identical training and test images would make the result misleading. We compared draft stakeholder statements against the shared case facts and removed extra customer commitments the model had invented.

We rejected an unsupported accuracy claim and a proposal to upload all footage for continuous improvement. Human work included defining acceptable defects, choosing the manual-check comparison and setting the trial's stopping conditions. We have verified the desk calculations and document consistency, not market demand or safety in a working mill.

## 3. Stakeholder Response Log

### 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received

**Investor:** Your subscription price and service cost are assumptions. A standard installation could support repeat sales, but travelling to solve unusual loom problems could consume the margin. I want a repeatable offer and a route to growth. Explain why your proposed custom defect definitions will not turn every customer into a separate engineering project.

**Customer (enterprise-buyer perspective):** I would prefer an agreed definition over a generic detector. Show the operator what triggered each alert, and retain the manual inspection route. A subscription is difficult to justify if nuisance alerts interrupt production. I would value reliable response to faults more than additional dashboard functions or promises about average accuracy.

**Supplier/Operations Partner:** We can discuss a standard mounting and replacement process, but loom access and lighting remain your responsibility until surveyed. Keep spare devices available if you promise quick replacement. Decide who pays for a second visit when the mill changes its arrangement. Equipment compatibility needs checking before you quote a fixed installation commitment.

**Regulator:** I cannot confirm which permissions or obligations apply to your proposed installation. You should seek qualified advice on workplace monitoring, data handling and machinery interfaces. Keeping processing local may reduce some exposure, but it does not settle these questions. Avoid presenting alerts as permission to alter safety procedures or bypass existing controls.

**Community/Ethics Stakeholder:** An operator may experience the same camera as supervision, even if you call it quality control. Excluding faces helps, but defect timestamps could still be linked to shifts. Let workers challenge an alert without being penalised. I favour less retained information; that conflicts with your buyer's wish for detailed evidence of every incident.

We prioritise operator acceptance and safe installation before sales growth because both are conditions for collecting meaningful trial evidence. We reject automatic loom stopping for this version: it adds an intervention risk without evidence that human review is too slow. We would reconsider only after a separate safety assessment and comparison of missed intervention costs. Customer requests for extended image retention are deferred until a trial identifies an actual investigative need and a proportionate retention period.

## 4. Decision Memo

Our judgement is **pivot** from general inspection automation to an operator-assisted service for repeated fabric runs. We recommend a limited trial design, subject to site and data review, rather than a production launch.

The strongest desk finding is also a warning. In an assumed set of 100 cloth segments, our constructed detector flags 18: 12 contain the defined defect and six do not. It misses three of the assumed 15 defective segments. These are illustrative counts, not measured performance. They show why a headline proportion conceals nuisance alerts and missed defects. We will compare actionable alerts, missed loss and operator time separately.

Commercially, our assumed RMB 420 monthly cost consists of RMB 120 equipment recovery, RMB 180 support, RMB 60 replacements and RMB 60 administration. At the assumed RMB 700 price the contribution is RMB 280. If support doubles, contribution falls to RMB 100. If the workable price is also RMB 550, contribution becomes negative RMB 50. Scaling would magnify that problem.

For the buyer, estimated avoidable loss must exceed the fee plus intervention effort. Using our assumed RMB 900 avoidable loss and RMB 100 extra operator effort gives only RMB 100 net benefit. We do not treat that as a sufficient purchasing case. We would seek a repeatable customer benefit of at least our assumed RMB 300 monthly buffer, with the customer's own valuation, before offering renewal.

The stakeholder conflict is between standardisation and order-specific reliability. We resolve it by restricting eligible orders and declining unsupported fabrics. This reduces initial sales possibilities while making the service promise more credible. We will compare a manual-check improvement and an alert-only device under the same defect definitions.

The ethical trade-off concerns diagnostic images. Short cloth-only excerpts can explain errors, but retention creates opportunities for shift surveillance. Our proposed default is local review and deletion after an assumed seven-day investigation window. Operators must be able to contest alerts; records will not identify individuals. This design requires consultation and technical checking before use.

Pause the trial if mounting cannot be made safe, privacy restrictions cannot be met or support demand exceeds the cost allowance. Stop pursuing this customer segment if agreed measurements show that better manual checks offer similar savings at materially lower cost. Remaining uncertainty includes defect rarity, fabric changes, acceptable interruption rates and willingness to pay. Each could overturn our recommendation.

### 5.3 Anticipated harms and response

The regulator's concern changes the product boundary: a camera may advise, but it will not actuate a loom. A physical isolation check and installer sign-off would precede connection. If safe mounting demands changes outside our competence, we would decline that site until an appropriate specialist can assess it. An unsigned checklist cannot substitute for that assessment.

The community concern reaches beyond whether a face appears. Production timestamps may permit indirect attribution. We would aggregate routine reporting by order, restrict detailed incident access and forbid worker ranking in customer terms. Before deployment, operators would see sample records and tell us what could identify them.

False alerts could also encourage wasteful rejection of acceptable cloth. We would record whether an operator's inspection confirmed each defect, then assess material losses against the manual baseline. Fewer defects in the detector's output do not by themselves prove less waste. Electricity use, replacement devices and any extra rejected cloth must be included before an environmental benefit is claimed.

### 5.4 Alignment thesis

Earlier, usable defect detection may reduce wasted fabric and give a mill a reason to renew. Both depend on the same condition: an alert changes a decision that avoids more loss than it creates. Device repair can also lower our costs. Privacy protection introduces preparation costs and limits diagnostic data, which we accept as part of the service price. We will claim alignment only where the trial records support the link between intervention, material saved and customer benefit.

## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt

**Opportunity Recognition. Judge:** Why would this mill buy when inspection already exists?

**Team 01:** Existing inspection may find a problem after more cloth has been woven. We would sell the value of earlier intervention on recurring defects. That is an opportunity hypothesis, not an observed purchasing pattern. Some orders tolerate the mark, and some mills already inspect frequently enough. Those would be poor initial customers. The proposed trial should identify the narrower group where timing changes a costly decision.

**Feasibility Judgement. Judge:** Which assumption would you investigate first?

**Team 01:** Whether an earlier alert actually prevents loss. We would agree defect definitions and compare against improved manual checking before building reporting features. In parallel, we need safe installation and data review because they determine whether the comparison can happen. If the benefit exists, support time and paid renewal become the next commercial tests.

**Stakeholder Reasoning. Judge:** Why did you disappoint the investor's request for standardisation?

**Team 01:** We accepted standard hardware, but a universal defect definition could make alerts irrelevant. A small, bounded setup step protects usefulness. If each order requires extensive retraining, our compromise fails and we should either raise the price for a viable segment or stop. The buyer's desired customisation does not automatically deserve funding. Logged setup time is the evidence that decides how much customisation we can offer.

**Ethical Positioning. Judge:** Could your privacy measures weaken the detector?

**Team 01:** Yes. Deleting images limits later diagnosis and training. We would accept that cost initially because unlimited retention is unnecessary to test the central proposition. If a specific fault requires a longer sample, we would define its purpose, access and deletion in advance. We cannot promise that every customer will agree. Losing a sale can be the consequence of maintaining the boundary.

**Venture Communication. Judge:** State your offer without an accuracy claim.

**Team 01:** We provide local cloth-defect alerts for selected repeated orders, with an operator deciding what to do. A cancellable trial compares avoidable loss and extra work with your existing inspection. We earn a subscription only if the measured benefit supports the fee. We still need to establish that benefit, so our next commitment is to a controlled comparison, not to a guaranteed saving.
