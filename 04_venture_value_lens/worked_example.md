# Case VVL-01: Campus Container Loop
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

This fictional reusable takeaway-container service would serve restaurants near a university campus in Suzhou. All venture details, quantities, costs, decisions and persona challenges below are constructed assumptions for teaching. No restaurant, student, judge or observer has participated. No pilot or stakeholder simulation is claimed to have occurred. The example demonstrates the lens only; it is not a complete seven-section student submission.

## Synthetic venture brief and assumptions

The service would supply reusable containers, collect returns, arrange washing and return clean stock to restaurants. Restaurants would pay per use. Diners would return containers to collection points. A refundable deposit is considered in Phase A and challenged later. The commercial hypothesis is that sufficiently frequent reuse and dense collection routes could support an affordable service while reducing disposable packaging. Environmental benefit remains untested because production, transport, washing, losses and end-of-life treatment all matter.

The fictional team would retain responsibility for defining and checking service controls even where washing is outsourced. It would not prepare food, set menus or purchase ingredients. Any campus collection space and restaurant participation would require future agreement.

| Assumption | Tested/untested | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurants might accept a service price of RMB 2.00 per completed use | Untested synthetic pricing assumption | Seek willingness-to-pay evidence and compare the complete service cost |
| A container might support 40 completed uses at a purchase cost of RMB 8.00 | Untested synthetic durability and price assumptions | Obtain product evidence and observe wear, rejection and loss in an appropriately controlled test |
| A planning volume of 1,000 completed uses would permit meaningful process costing | Untested synthetic volume assumption | Estimate demand and record actual task time, route length and throughput in a later test |
| Safe washing and handling can be specified and verified | Untested synthetic feasibility assumption | Obtain qualified technical review and define validation before any live food-contact use |

All amounts below are synthetic planning estimates, not expenditure, quotations or project finance records.

## Phase A worksheet: steps 1 to 5

**Classify.** Restaurants (FB-RN) is the selected SASB industry because the service supports takeaway food operations and depends on restaurant handling and customer trust. It is a proxy: the venture itself is a container service rather than a meal provider. No secondary industry is selected in this bounded example. Reconsider the mapping if manufacturing or another activity becomes substantial. The Restaurants industry description and selection principles are in SASB (2018g, pp. 4 to 5).

**Read the topic list.** The standard's contents page lists Energy Management; Water Management; Food & Packaging Waste Management; Food Safety; Nutritional Content; Labor Practices; Supply Chain Management & Food Sourcing (SASB, 2018g, p. 3). The source spelling “Labor” is retained in the label.

**Select.** Energy Management matters because washing and collection consume energy. Water Management matters because washing adds a resource dependency. Food & Packaging Waste Management concerns the intended benefit and the cost of lost or damaged stock. Food Safety concerns contamination through repeated use and handling. Labor Practices concerns workload, paid handling time and who bears the pressure of returns. These five topics are selected from the standard. Nutritional Content and Supply Chain Management & Food Sourcing are omitted because the proposed service does not control recipes or buy ingredients. This exclusion does not remove responsibility for container procurement or washing partners; those dependencies remain in the selected topics. No venture-specific sixth topic is added. Deposit access is addressed under anticipated harms.

**Rate and check.** The Phase A position is deliberately incomplete:

| Topic | Exposure (L/M/H) + reason | Readiness (0 to 3) + mechanism | Gap? | Relationship | Weight + reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy Management | M: washing and collection require energy; usage is unknown | 1: intention to operate efficiently, with no owned batch or route control | No: M needs ≥1 | Neutral: an energy benefit or disadvantage has not been established | Medium: recurring operating impact from launch, scale uncertain |
| Water Management | M: washing is integral; local scarcity is not established | 1: intention to minimise water use without a measurement plan | No: M needs ≥1 | Hidden risk: unmeasured washing demand could raise cost and weaken the benefit claim | Medium: immediate recurring demand, with uncertain magnitude |
| Food & Packaging Waste Management | H: loss and early disposal could defeat the core model | 2: operations lead designs stock reconciliation and rejection records; synthetic purchase allocation RMB 0.20 per use plus RMB 0.15 loss allowance | No: H needs ≥2 | Reinforcing, conditional: repeated use could reduce discarded packaging and replacement cost | High: core value proposition and economics depend on this from launch |
| Food Safety | H: contamination could harm diners through repeated food contact | 1: intent to use a washing partner; acceptance criteria and responsibilities unspecified | Yes: H needs ≥2; specific, owned and costed control missing | Hidden risk: an unmanaged safety dependency could harm diners and interrupt service | High: potentially serious harm from the first food-contact use |
| Labor Practices | M: collection and cleaning create repeated handling work | 0: no workload or paid-time provision | Yes: M needs ≥1 | Hidden risk: unplanned labour could shift costs to workers and undermine delivery | Medium: recurring workload begins immediately; actual task times unknown |

Food Safety and Labor Practices are explicit gaps. Water Management meets the minimum while retaining a material uncertainty. A gap-free table would still require scrutiny of the proposed controls.

## Constructed Phase B challenges

These are synthetic persona prompts for illustrating revision, not records of a simulation run or statements by external individuals.

**Investor.** The service price may not cover labour, losses and the upfront cost of a safe process. Show what would change the decision to proceed.

**Customer.** Restaurants may value reliable availability and trusted cleaning more than environmental language. Collection delays could disrupt service.

**Supplier / Operations Partner.** Washing batches and collections need clear acceptance rules, realistic capacity and allocated time. A low price cannot establish these conditions.

**Regulator.** Repeated food contact may raise hygiene and handling questions. Identify applicable requirements with an appropriate authority or qualified adviser, and establish validation before live use. This prompt gives no compliance determination.

**Community / Ethics Stakeholder.** A deposit could exclude some diners or make refunds burdensome. Reuse could also move environmental burden into washing and transport. Explain whose interests have priority if cost increases.

## 5. Venture Value Map

This is the proposed Phase C revision within the synthetic example. The changed designs remain untested.

### 5.1 Industry mapping

Restaurants (FB-RN), Version 2018-10, remains the customer-context proxy. The service still does not prepare food. The same five topics and omission reasons are retained; see SASB (2018g, pp. 3 to 5). Revised mechanisms address collection, washing, inventory and paid work without claiming to implement the standard's accounting metrics.

### 5.2 Material topics

| Topic | Exposure (L/M/H) + reason | Readiness (0 to 3) + mechanism | Gap? | Relationship | Weight + reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy Management | M: same washing and route dependency; controls do not lower exposure | 2: operations lead specifies full-batch scheduling and route consolidation; allocate RMB 0.10 per use for energy and RMB 0.35 for collection, plus shared logging time below | No: M needs ≥1 | Reinforcing, conditional: lower energy per completed use could reduce resource demand and cost; test against actual batch and route data | Medium: recurring impact from launch; magnitude depends on utilisation |
| Water Management | M: washing remains necessary; no Suzhou scarcity claim | 2: operations lead specifies separate batch-volume records and a review before route expansion; allocate RMB 0.05 per use for water and shared logging time | No: M needs ≥1 | Trade-off: safe washing consumes water and margin; the venture carries this cost within the restaurant service price | Medium: immediate repeated resource use; compare against the displaced option before claiming net benefit |
| Food & Packaging Waste Management | H: repeated use, loss and disposal determine the core model | 2: operations lead reconciles stock each collection cycle and quarantines damaged units; allocate RMB 0.20 per use for purchase recovery and RMB 0.15 for loss, with shared logging time | No: H needs ≥2 | Reinforcing, conditional: fewer discarded containers and lower replacement cost depend on sufficient safe reuse; repeated-use evidence is absent | High: central environmental claim and cost dependency from launch |
| Food Safety | H: potential diner harm persists even with planned controls | 2: safety lead designs clean/dirty separation, batch traceability, hold-and-release records and a stop-use trigger for failed checks; allocate RMB 0.25 per use for cleaning consumables and checks, plus RMB 1,500 upfront for qualified review and validation planning | No: H needs ≥2; design threshold met, effectiveness untested | Trade-off: stronger controls cost margin; the venture funds them and pauses live food-contact use until validation | High: potentially serious harm at first use; this takes priority over expansion |
| Labor Practices | M: repeated handling and time pressure remain | 2: operations lead specifies paid handling time, a workload review after each trial session and no unpaid overflow; allocate RMB 0.60 per use plus shared logging time | No: M needs ≥1 | Trade-off: paid handling and realistic capacity reduce margin; the venture bears the cost rather than assuming free restaurant or student labour | Medium: working conditions and reliability matter immediately, while workload estimates remain uncertain |

**Cost basis and limits.** At the assumed 1,000 uses, the synthetic variable allocations total RMB 1.70 per use, leaving RMB 0.30 before shared monitoring, upfront costs and other overhead. The labour allowance assumes two minutes at RMB 18 per hour solely for fictional arithmetic, with no claim about local pay requirements or market rates. Shared monitoring is estimated at two hours per 1,000 uses at the same assumed rate, costing RMB 36, counted once across the controls. The RMB 1,500 review and validation-planning allowance alone exceeds the remaining RMB 264. This is not a complete break-even model. Prices, actual task time, other overhead and the cost and adequacy of technical validation need evidence. Specifying a budget supports readiness 2; it does not prove affordability or safety.

**Testing and decision.** No row earns readiness 3. A non-food return-flow test could measure route time, losses, user burden and task workload. It cannot validate food-contact safety or environmental superiority. Pause live food-contact deployment until relevant technical requirements, control validation and complete cost assumptions are resolved. Revisit the price or operating model if safe delivery cannot be funded. Compare lifecycle burdens before making a net environmental-benefit claim.

The restaurant-level agreement is a proposed design, not a negotiated commitment. These responses would be traced in a full submission's stakeholder response log using the same decision labels and evidence conditions.

### 5.4 Alignment thesis

Repeated container use may reduce disposable packaging and replacement cost if return rates and useful life are sufficient. Safe washing and paid handling reduce margin, which the venture must fund through its restaurant price. Removing the proposed deposit may improve access while increasing stock-loss exposure. We will test return logistics without food contact, pause live deployment until safety controls are validated, and compare environmental burdens before claiming a net benefit. The current design meets the lens's readiness thresholds but has no tested mechanisms. Evidence on costs, return behaviour, safety and lifecycle impacts could justify a price change, a pivot or stopping.

## What the example demonstrates

Ethical Positioning is visible in the safety stop and explicit allocation of costs. Stakeholder Reasoning is visible in the response to diner access and restaurant stock risk. Feasibility Judgement is visible in the unresolved cost shortfall and the distinction between a logistics test and a live food-contact service.
