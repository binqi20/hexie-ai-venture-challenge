# Facilitator note: a 20-minute Venture Value Lens exercise

Design guidance, prepared 5 October 2026. The first live run is scheduled for early December 2026; this note makes no claim of participant use or learning outcomes. Bring the worksheet, map template, industry topic list and selected standards. Preselect likely industry documents so that the exercise does not become a file search. Explain that readiness and relationship labels are project rules, with source principles documented in [value_lens_method.md](value_lens_method.md).

## Phase A: 20 minutes

| Minutes | Facilitation task | Team output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Establish the proposed activity, customer and revenue model. Ask why the closest industry fits and where it fails. | Industry and SICS code, source and mapping limitation |
| 3 to 6 | Choose 3 to 5 disclosure topics; permit one justified venture-specific addition. Ask who is affected and through which activity. | Topics with materiality reasons and omissions |
| 6 to 11 | Rate exposure before controls, then readiness. Ask who owns each mechanism and what resources it needs. | Reasoned exposure and readiness with evidence status |
| 11 to 14 | Apply High ≥2, Medium ≥1, Low no minimum. Require every shortfall to remain visible. | Gap list, next action and owner |
| 14 to 18 | Classify relationships and weight by impact and timing. Identify anticipated Regulator and Community / Ethics Stakeholder concerns. | First material-topics table and harm questions |
| 18 to 20 | Draft the alignment thesis in no more than 120 words. Ask what evidence would change the decision. | Saved Phase A Venture Value Map |

Use the reusable-container example if teams need an illustration. It is SYNTHETIC and provides no operational evidence. Keep this exercise focused on a defensible first map; unresolved facts remain explicit.

## Common errors and facilitator prompts

**ESG vocabulary without mechanisms.** Ask: “What changes in practice, who owns it, and what resources are allocated?” A sustainability promise is readiness 1. A plan to test is not readiness 3.

**Low exposure chosen to avoid work.** Ask: “What activity, customer, supply-chain or location evidence supports Low before controls?” Outsourcing does not remove the dependency. Unknown is not evidence of low exposure.

**Every topic called Reinforcing.** Ask: “How does the social benefit create revenue or a cost advantage, who pays, and under what conditions?” Require a plausible pathway and a test. Retain Trade-off, Neutral or Hidden risk where appropriate; do not require artificial balance across labels.

**No gap mistaken for proof.** Ask what remains untested. A designed safety control can meet the readiness threshold while still requiring validation before use. Weights cannot cancel a gap.

## Phase C: revisit after the simulation

Retrieve the saved Phase A map and actual Phase B response log. Revisit the same topics in light of challenges, especially those from the Regulator and Community / Ethics Stakeholder. Distinguish a persona claim, a team assumption and verified evidence. Record ADOPT, PARTIALLY ADOPT, REJECT or DEFER with a reason and evidence that would change the decision.

Update exposure only when the business or contextual evidence changes. Update readiness when the mechanism or its evidence changes. Revise relationships, weights and the thesis, and trace consequential changes to the decision memo's proceed / pivot / pause / stop judgement. Judge the reasoning under Ethical Positioning, Stakeholder Reasoning and Feasibility Judgement. There is no additional cluster, letter rating or reward for polished ESG language. Preserve both map versions so that changes can be inspected.
