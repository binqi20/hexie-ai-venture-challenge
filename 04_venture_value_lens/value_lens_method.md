# Venture Value Lens: ESG-derived method

Version 1.0, October 2026. Design resource for early-stage ventures; no claim of observed student outcomes. Read alongside the [reference list](../REFERENCES.md). All source page numbers below are one-based PDF pages.

## Purpose and boundaries

The lens helps a team examine commercial value and social value together before it has an operating history. Commercial value concerns adoption, revenue, costs and the ability to continue operating. Social value concerns benefits and harms for people and the environment, including those who do not pay the venture. Alignment means explaining a mechanism through which these interests support each other, or making a conflict and its allocation explicit. A profitable activity can still impose harm on others.

MSCI frames ESG issues as potential costs and opportunities for a company (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 5). SASB identifies industry-specific sustainability issues relevant to operating performance and financial condition (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board [SASB], 2018g, pp. 4 to 5). These investor-oriented starting points help locate dependencies. The project extends the discussion to affected stakeholders, fairness and harms even when a financial consequence is uncertain. This extension is a teaching design choice, not a claim that financial materiality measures all social value.

The output is a reasoned Venture Value Map. It is evidence for Ethical Positioning, Stakeholder Reasoning and Feasibility Judgement. It is not a sixth capability cluster and never produces a letter rating. Industry peer normalisation requires comparative data that these ventures lack, and aggregation could conceal an unresolved safety issue behind stronger performance elsewhere. Readiness levels and topic weights organise discussion; they are neither investment ratings nor a mathematical student grade. SASB provides disclosure standards, not a venture rating system.

## 1. Classify

**Adapted first principle: industry-specific materiality.** SASB groups standards by SICS industry and allows a company to consider additional industries where its activities warrant this (SASB, 2018g, pp. 4 to 5).

Map the venture to the closest SASB SICS industry. Explain the fit through what the venture does, whom it serves and how it earns revenue. Name a secondary industry if it materially changes the analysis. Use the industry description, not the module label alone. A venture selling to restaurants may use Restaurants as a customer-context proxy while acknowledging that it does not prepare meals. An imperfect fit must remain visible.

## 2. Select material topics

**Adapted first principle: material issues depend on the business, including features that differ from industry peers.** SASB assigns responsibility for selecting relevant topics to the reporting company and recognises that an industry standard may not cover every material issue (SASB, 2018g, p. 5). MSCI permits company-specific key issues when the business or its exposure differs (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 14 to 15).

Choose the 3 to 5 disclosure topics most material to the venture's proposed model. For each, explain the affected stakeholder, the activity creating exposure and the potential commercial consequence or social harm. Optionally add one venture-specific topic, explicitly labelled as a project addition, with a reason the standard misses it. Record why apparently relevant topics were omitted. This follows the principle of explaining omissions in SASB's application guidance (SASB, 2018h, p. 2). The number of topics and permission for one addition are project rules, not SASB requirements.

## 3. Rate exposure

**Adapted first principle: separate exposure from the quality of management.** MSCI considers business activity, geography and company characteristics; FTSE considers the relevance of themes through industrial activity and operational context (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 18 to 19, 21; FTSE Russell, 2021, pp. 6 to 8).

Assign Low, Medium or High exposure to each topic, with a reason tied to the business model, customers, supply chain and location. High indicates a central dependency or potentially substantial harm; Medium indicates a meaningful but more bounded dependency; Low indicates limited exposure with an explicit explanation. These descriptions support qualitative judgement, not estimated probabilities. Do not assume that Suzhou has a particular water-risk tier or regulatory requirement without evidence.

Assess exposure before giving credit for controls. Outsourcing a hazardous activity or promising to manage it does not remove the dependency. A change to the actual business model may change exposure; a better control normally changes readiness. Where information is missing, record the uncertainty and what would establish a defensible exposure judgement. Lack of evidence is no reason to select Low.

## 4. Rate management readiness

**Adapted first principle: distinguish organisational commitment, implemented initiatives and performance evidence.** MSCI separates these elements in its management assessment. SASB also asks for contextual accounts of governance, strategy, risk processes and metrics (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 22; SASB, 2018h, p. 3).

Use the project's pre-launch readiness scale. **0 None** means that no relevant response is identified. **1 Stated intent** means that the team has a commitment but lacks a sufficiently specified mechanism. **2 Designed mechanism** requires a specific action or control, an accountable owner and an explicit cost or resource allocation. A cost estimate is acceptable if its basis and uncertainty are stated. **3 Tested mechanism** requires a pilot or other evidence that tests the relevant mechanism. Identify the evidence, what it tested, the result and its limits. A plan to pilot does not earn level 3. A simulation of a stakeholder challenge does not establish that an operational control works.

A designed mechanism should specify its trigger and follow-up as well as ownership. Record projected quantities as assumptions. SASB permits substantiated estimates and discussion of uncertainty; this does not turn an unsupported forecast into observed performance (SASB, 2018h, p. 3). The four readiness levels are the project's adaptation, not a reproduction of an ESG provider's scoring scale.

## 5. Apply exposure-scaled expectations

**Adapted first principle: greater exposure warrants stronger management.** FTSE uses higher thresholds for companies with higher theme exposure. MSCI similarly links required management to exposure and retains a minimum-management feature in its risk model (FTSE Russell, 2021, pp. 4, 11; MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 18, 31).

Apply the project rule exactly: High exposure needs readiness at least 2; Medium needs at least 1; Low has no minimum. A shortfall is a flagged gap. Record the missing mechanism, owner, next action and evidence needed to revisit the gap. A High topic at readiness 1 is a gap even if its mission is attractive. A Low topic at readiness 0 passes this particular check, without establishing that the venture is harmless.

These thresholds are educational design choices. They do not reproduce FTSE bands or MSCI formulas, and the Low rule is a deliberate simplification of MSCI's minimum-management logic. Meeting a threshold does not establish regulatory compliance, prove effectiveness or resolve every stakeholder concern.

## 6. Classify the commercial and social value relationship

**Adapted first principle: sustainability issues can create opportunities as well as risks.** MSCI distinguishes these routes and recognises that management affects the ability to realise an opportunity (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 5, 32 to 33).

Assign one primary relationship per topic, then explain the mechanism and its conditions. **Reinforcing** means that social value drives a revenue or cost advantage. Name the beneficiary, the commercial route and the evidence needed to test it. **Neutral** means that no material connection is currently established within the stated scope; it does not mean the topic is ethically irrelevant. **Trade-off** means that social value costs margin; state who pays, who benefits and why that allocation is defensible. **Hidden risk** means that unmanaged exposure could become a cost. Identify the missing control and plausible consequence.

These four labels are project categories. They can change when the business design or evidence changes. Where several relationships coexist, state the dominant one and retain the secondary tension in the explanation. Do not label every row Reinforcing merely because the venture has a social mission.

## 7. Weight topics

**Adapted first principle: priority depends on impact and time horizon.** MSCI considers the industry's contribution to an externality and the expected timing of its financial consequences (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 15 to 17).

Assign High, Medium or Low weight, explaining both the magnitude or seriousness of the potential impact and when it may arise. For this project, consider stakeholder harm as well as the venture's consequences. A potentially severe future harm may warrant High weight despite a longer horizon. Weights set attention and sequencing; they are not percentages, are not summed and cannot offset a flagged gap. Distinguish weight, which sets priority, from exposure, which identifies the venture's involvement in the issue.

## 8. Identify anticipated harms and responses

**Adapted first principle: adverse impacts can reveal weaknesses in management.** MSCI includes controversy evidence in evaluating a company's record (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 22). Early ventures lack that history. The project's forward-looking substitute is an anticipated-harm discussion, not a controversy deduction.

In Phase A, identify plausible harms and prepare questions for the Regulator and Community / Ethics Stakeholder personas. In Phase B, record the harms these personas actually raise within the simulation. In Phase C, explain the team's response, residual risk and evidence that would change the decision. Use ADOPT, PARTIALLY ADOPT, REJECT or DEFER with a reason. Persona statements are challenges to examine, not regulatory determinations or observed community feedback. In a synthetic demonstration, label the challenges as constructed material.

## 9. Write the alignment thesis

**Adapted first principle: connect material sustainability issues to strategy, risk management and value creation.** SASB asks for narrative context linking risks and opportunities to governance, strategy and metrics; MSCI explicitly considers costs and opportunities (SASB, 2018h, p. 3; MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 5).

Write one paragraph of no more than 120 words. Explain where commercial and social value reinforce or conflict, what the venture will do, who bears the costs, which gaps remain and what evidence could change the judgement. This synthesis is a project requirement. It should support a proceed / pivot / pause / stop judgement without implying that a well-written thesis proves the venture's claims.

## How the map feeds assessment

For **Ethical Positioning**, assess whether the team recognises harms, rights, fairness and sustainability, compares alternatives and defends concrete responses. For **Stakeholder Reasoning**, assess whether it prioritises affected parties, explains conflicts and dependencies, and gives reasons for adopting, rejecting or deferring feedback. For **Feasibility Judgement**, assess whether the team connects exposure, readiness gaps, resource requirements and uncertainty to its decision. Use the existing rubric descriptors. A readiness 3 is evidence to inspect, not an automatic Advanced judgement. Record the distinct reasoning supporting each cluster and avoid awarding credit simply for ESG vocabulary or a full table.

## What does not transfer

**Peer-relative normalisation** does not transfer because comparable operating peers and consistent data are unavailable. Use the project's anchored judgements, with no industry rank or letter grade (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, pp. 50 to 52; FTSE Russell, 2021, p. 4).

**Controversy deductions** do not transfer because an unlaunched venture has no comparable operating record. Anticipated harms remain scenarios to address, rather than alleged incidents or point deductions (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 22).

**Governance deduction models** do not transfer. Founder responsibilities and decision rights can inform a mechanism, but the lens does not apply listed-company board, ownership or pay deductions (MSCI ESG Research LLC, 2022a, p. 5).

**Audited performance metrics** do not transfer as a requirement or a claim. Use labelled estimates and bounded test evidence appropriate to the venture. This exclusion does not mean that all source metrics require audit: SASB discusses uncertainty and estimates and makes seeking third-party assurance optional (SASB, 2018h, p. 3).

**Public-data-only rules** do not transfer. The lens may use submitted plans, disclosed assumptions and relevant test records, with their provenance and limits. This explicitly departs from the public-information approach described in FTSE Russell's product overview (FTSE Russell, 2020, p. 1).

## HeXie note: the project's interpretation

In this lens, **He** means aligning stakeholder value. **Xie** means designing mechanisms that make that alignment reliable, represented by specified ownership, resources, controls and testing. This is the project's interpretation, set out in the [HeXie design note](../00_design/hexie_design_logic.md); it is not a definition attributed to the ESG sources or an empirical finding about learning. Its practical test is whether the team can explain how a proposed alignment would hold when stakeholder interests conflict.
