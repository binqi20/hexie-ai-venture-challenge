# Responsible AI plan

Version 1.0, October 2026. Operating design for the first live run in early December 2026. No participant data have been collected yet. Safeguards below are requirements for delivery, not claims that a live deployment has passed them.

**Human-led pedagogy, AI-assisted simulation, no AI-only decisions about students**

## Responsibility and boundaries

The project leader owns the activity design, ethics alignment, and readiness decision. Human facilitators review and moderate stakeholder output, explain limitations, provide access support, and may pause, revise, or discard output. Human assessors apply the five capability clusters and resolve scoring disagreements. AI generates simulated perspectives; it does not award scores, select winners, decide misconduct, or make module assessment decisions. Research data must not be repurposed for individual grading, progression, or profiling.

December participation is voluntary and separate from module assessment. Participation in the activity, permission to use data for research, optional research use of AI interaction records, and optional future reuse are distinct choices. A participant can stop AI interaction without giving a reason. Research non-consent does not affect eligibility for the activity or any announced recognition.

## Stage-specific AI boundaries

| Stage | Permitted role and human responsibility |
|---|---|
| Phase A | AI may support ideation, critique, drafting, or language assistance. Teams verify consequential claims, distinguish assumptions from evidence, and submit the Stage 1 declaration. |
| Phase B | The moderated panel generates role-based challenges from shared facts. Teams decide ADOPT, PARTIALLY ADOPT, REJECT, or DEFER and explain evidence that would change the decision. |
| Phase C | AI may support revision and preparation, with a final declaration. Teams own the recommendation. Defence answers express their own reasoning; agreed assistive communication remains available. |
| Research evaluation | Human researchers select consented, minimal evidence and interpret it within the four approved streams. This plan does not authorise uploading participant research records to an external AI system. |

## Risk register

Likelihood and impact are prospective design judgements, not measured incident rates. Low, Medium, and High indicate relative concern for this activity. Owners are required roles to assign before the run.

## Output checks and intervention

Use the simulation rule: shared facts, different stakeholder utilities. Conflict should arise from priorities, risk tolerance, time horizons, and claims. A persona may question an assumption; it may not invent market research, regulations, interviews, or test results to win an argument. Label facts from the supplied case, team assumptions, and simulated concerns distinctly. Externally check consequential factual claims before relying on them; if verification is unavailable, keep them explicitly uncertain.

The facilitator checks each output before showing it where practicable. Remove personal attacks, stereotypes, unsafe instructions, and unsupported claims of authority. For a materially false premise already shown, pause, correct the premise for all affected teams, and allow reasonable revision time. Do not penalise students for the system's error. Keep a minimal incident note describing the failure and remedy without unnecessary participant details.

For an adverse event or accidental disclosure, stop the affected interaction, restrict further access, and alert the project leader. Report adverse events to the approving research ethics committee as soon as possible. Let authorised institutional staff direct any data incident response. Do not copy sensitive content into this repository or an AI prompt to explain the incident.

## Accessibility and alternative participation

Provide the brief and rubric in readable, accessible text, with meaningful headings and tables that do not depend on colour. Explain the task in plain language, offer written questions and agreed communication support, and allow preparation time and breaks. Check access needs privately without collecting diagnoses in research or scoring files. Prior AI familiarity and premium subscriptions are not prerequisites.

The non-AI route uses facilitator-authored stakeholder cards based on the same fact sheet and five roles. The facilitator presents conflicting priorities, takes clarifying questions, and offers equivalent challenge depth. Teams complete the same response log, memo, map, declarations (accurately stating non-use), and defence. Provide paper or accessible digital copies and equivalent time. A facilitator-mediated route can remove the need for a personal AI account, but students choosing no AI should receive the human-authored cards. Research participation remains separately optional on either route.

## Tool and platform principles

Use an institutionally supported platform whose suitability for this activity has been confirmed. Record the actual service, access route, relevant version and date, data destinations, permitted users, retention settings, training-use terms, and deletion controls in restricted operational records. Confirm these from current institutional and platform documentation before December; subscription, API access, or local operation alone does not establish approval or complete privacy protection.

Never enter personal, sensitive, confidential, or identifiable information into the simulation. Exclude names, student IDs, contact details, grades, health information, financial information, and private organisational details. Do not expose unpublished proprietary venture content; reduce it to a safe description or use the alternative route. No participant records belong in this reusable repository.

Explain that third-party services may process or retain input under their own policies and that the research team does not control their servers or retention. Do not promise provider-side deletion or zero retention without verified support. Disable avoidable logging and use only the minimum input. Do not add connectors, external sharing, or new data flows without institutional review of their suitability.

**Confirm locally before use:** the platform, institutional acceptance, processing locations, current terms, and retention/deletion settings. Verification requires a dated operational record and confirmation from the relevant institutional service owner. This document names no selected supplier and makes no current legal-compliance claim.

## Participant briefing

“This panel simulates stakeholder perspectives. Its output may be inaccurate, incomplete, or biased. Human facilitators and assessors remain responsible for the activity and evaluation. Explain which suggestions you accept or reject and why. Do not enter personal, sensitive, confidential, or identifying information. You may stop the AI interaction and request the alternative route. Research participation is optional and will not affect module marks, academic standing, relationships with staff, or eligibility for the activity.”
