# HeXie-AI Venture Challenge competition playbook

This playbook specifies the proposed operating format for the first live run in early December 2026, date to be confirmed. It records no completed live event. Session timings below are proposed facilitation choices for this release.

## Purpose and learning logic

Teams develop a venture, receive conflicting stakeholder feedback, revise their proposal and defend their decisions. The purpose is to make entrepreneurial judgement visible through linked evidence. AI can support preparation; students remain responsible for evaluating claims, deciding which feedback matters and explaining the result. This design may support practice in judgement under uncertainty. Learning effects require evaluation.

The competition combines the capability rubric with the moderated Stakeholder-Agent Swarm Simulation. The [HeXie design note](../00_design/hexie_design_logic.md) explains the project's interpretation of structured processes and conditions for human initiative. The rubric assesses Opportunity Recognition (25%), Feasibility Judgement (25%), Stakeholder Reasoning (20%), Ethical Positioning (15%) and Venture Communication (15%). Use the [capability_rubric.md](../02_assessment_kit/capability_rubric.md) descriptors at Advanced, Proficient, Developing and Emerging levels. Polish, charisma, visual design and AI fluency earn no credit by themselves.

## Phase A: venture development

Teams identify a specific problem and customer, explain their solution and business model, and separate evidence from assumptions. Produce a venture brief of no more than 400 words with the assumption table specified below. Use AI for ideation, analysis, drafting or language support within the published task boundaries. Verify factual claims against accessible sources and label untested assumptions. A proposed test is not a completed test.

Complete the Stage 1 AI-use declaration, no more than 200 words. Record the tools, purposes, outputs, verification and material changes or rejections. Construct the first Venture Value Map using the [materiality worksheet](../04_venture_value_lens/materiality_worksheet.md) and [value-alignment template](../04_venture_value_lens/value_alignment_map_template.md). Retain this baseline so that later changes remain visible. The facilitator reviews completeness before the panel session; this check supplies no model answer.

## Phase B: stakeholder simulation

Use a 90-minute session, following the [facilitator run sheet](facilitator_run_sheet.md). Give all five roles the same venture brief, assumptions and permitted evidence. Prepare independent role responses and check them before release. Present them in this order:

1. Investor: viability, scalability, execution risk and return.
2. Customer: problem fit, trust, willingness to pay and adoption.
3. Supplier / Operations Partner: deliverability, dependence, terms and capacity.
4. Regulator: compliance questions, procedural legitimacy and public safety, using non-authoritative language.
5. Community / Ethics Stakeholder: access, distributional effects, sustainability and local impact.

Use the lender / green-finance variant for relevant financing questions and the enterprise-buyer variant for business-customer scrutiny. Retain the five-role structure. Record any variant in the session materials. The [simulation design](../03_simulation_kit/simulation_design.md), [personas](../03_simulation_kit/personas.yaml) and [prompts](../03_simulation_kit/prompts.md) supply role detail.

The rule is shared facts, different stakeholder utilities. Conflict arises through priorities, risk tolerances, time horizons and claims. An invented law, statistic or customer finding is an output error requiring correction. The hidden moderator checks format, evidence and role boundaries; the human facilitator decides whether feedback is suitable for release. AI feedback is provisional and non-authoritative. It cannot establish market demand, regulatory permission or a correct venture decision.

Teams summarise challenges in the stakeholder response log, then make decisions. Clarification can expose a priority or an assumption; it must not quietly introduce new evidence for only some teams. Record a correction and distribute it to every affected team. If the tool fails, use facilitator-reviewed text responses with the same evidence rules and disclose the delivery change.

## Decision logic and Phase C

For each material stakeholder point, use `ADOPT`, `PARTIALLY ADOPT`, `REJECT` or `DEFER`. Explain the reason and the evidence that would change the decision. Partial adoption must specify what changes and what remains. Deferral must identify the unresolved issue and the needed evidence. Rejection requires a reason grounded in the venture's circumstances. Neither the volume of accepted feedback nor the number of revisions earns automatic credit.

Select the principal stakeholder conflict and explain whose concerns carry weight, which alternatives were considered and what uncertainty remains. Review the Venture Value Map for changes in materiality, exposure, readiness and anticipated harms. The Lens supplies evidence for Ethical Positioning, Stakeholder Reasoning and Feasibility Judgement; it creates no additional cluster or letter rating.

Revise the venture and write a one-page decision memo, maximum 500 words. State a feasibility judgement of proceed, pivot, pause or stop. A well-supported pause or stop can demonstrate strong judgement. Complete the final AI-use declaration, maximum 150 words, identifying where human judgement is most visible and accepting responsibility. Prepare a short presentation and a structured oral defence lasting 8 to 12 minutes.

## Submission pack and exact headings

Use one integrated pack headed `# <Venture name>`. Preserve the headings below exactly. Keep both initial and revised venture briefs and maps, clearly labelled by phase, so that revision is inspectable. The initial versions are process evidence within the same pack, not new scored components.

| Required heading | Required contents and template |
|---|---|
| `## 1. Venture Brief` | Maximum 400 words per version: problem, target customer, solution, business model; table `assumption \| tested/untested \| how to test`. No separate venture-brief template is specified. |
| `## 2. AI-Use Declaration: Stage 1` | Maximum 200 words; tools, purposes, AI output, verification, changes or rejections. Use [ai_use_declaration.md](../02_assessment_kit/ai_use_declaration.md). |
| `## 3. Stakeholder Response Log` | Use [stakeholder_response_log_template.md](../02_assessment_kit/stakeholder_response_log_template.md). |
| `### 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received` | One short paragraph per role, in the five-role order above. |
| `### 3.2 Team decisions` | Table: `Stakeholder \| Key point \| Decision \| Reason \| Evidence that would change our decision`. |
| `## 4. Decision Memo` | One page, maximum 500 words: opportunity; key evidence; feasibility judgement; main stakeholder conflict and resolution; ethical trade-off; final recommendation; remaining uncertainty. Use [decision_memo_template.md](../02_assessment_kit/decision_memo_template.md). |
| `## 5. Venture Value Map` | First and revised maps, using the [value_alignment_map_template.md](../04_venture_value_lens/value_alignment_map_template.md) and [materiality_worksheet.md](../04_venture_value_lens/materiality_worksheet.md). |
| `### 5.1 Industry mapping` | Closest SASB SICS industry, with a secondary industry if relevant. |
| `### 5.2 Material topics` | Columns: topic; exposure with reason; readiness with mechanism; gap; relationship; weight with reason. |
| `### 5.3 Anticipated harms and response` | Identify harms and the team's mechanisms or outstanding questions. |
| `### 5.4 Alignment thesis` | Maximum 120 words on relationships between commercial and social value. |
| `## 6. AI-Use Declaration: Final` | Maximum 150 words, including visible human judgement and responsibility. Use [ai_use_declaration.md](../02_assessment_kit/ai_use_declaration.md). |
| `## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt` | Five actual judge questions, one per cluster, with team answers. Use [oral_defence_guide.md](../02_assessment_kit/oral_defence_guide.md) for prompts. Complete after the defence from agreed notes; never fabricate an exchange. |

The presentation accompanies the pack. The proposed run sheet allocates three minutes to it, followed by a separate ten-minute defence. At the pre-defence deadline, section 7 remains pending; complete it after the session. Submit all live materials only through the approved institutional route. Participant submissions and transcripts never enter this repository.

## Oral defence and judge workflow

Before the December event, three human assessors undertake calibration using [judge_calibration_guide.md](../02_assessment_kit/judge_calibration_guide.md). Publish the rubric and question domains in advance. Read each team's initial and revised artefacts, noting evidence relevant to each cluster and points needing explanation. Apply one agreed timing and questioning protocol, with documented accessibility adjustments.

During the defence, ask one question per cluster and use follow-ups to clarify evidence, assumptions or trade-offs. Invite contributions across team members. The default is a team defence, consistent with the canonical team answers. It cannot establish every member's individual attainment. Students answer without live generative-AI assistance; agreed assistive technologies and communication adjustments remain available. Explain this distinction before the event.

Judge the pack and answers together. Record evidence-based cluster judgements independently before moderation, using the published rubric and scoring note. Resolve differences by examining evidence and descriptors. Do not infer competence from accent, confidence, design polish or agreement with AI. Provide a short reasoned feedback record and invite judge-usability feedback through the approved instrument. A future credit-bearing adaptation needs its own approved assessment arrangements.

## Facilitation, inclusion and accessibility

Provide readable digital materials, plain-language instructions and written access to each stakeholder response. Share the sequence and question domains early. Invite adjustment requests privately through the approved route. Offer a facilitator-mediated route using the same reviewed panel text so that participation does not require a personal paid AI subscription. Protect thinking time and allow students to state uncertainty without penalty for candour.

Use role-based personas without demographic caricatures. Interrupt abusive, discriminatory or overly authoritative feedback. Explain the correction without making the team responsible for the system error. Keep examples and submissions free of unnecessary personal or confidential information. Confirm consent procedures before collecting evaluation data. The project leader must settle collection, access and retention arrangements through the responsible AI documents; this playbook grants no additional data permission.
