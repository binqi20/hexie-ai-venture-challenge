# Facilitator run sheet

Proposed operating script for the first live run in early December 2026, date to be confirmed. This is a delivery design, not a record of a completed event. The 90-minute simulation is followed by a separately scheduled presentation and oral defence. Timings are elapsed minutes; intervals are contiguous and end at the next interval's start.

## Before students arrive

Confirm the event brief, consent arrangements, accessibility adjustments and approved institutional submission route. Read the [competition playbook](competition_playbook.md) and [responsible AI plan](../06_responsible_ai/responsible_ai_plan.md), and review your institution's data-protection and research-ethics requirements. Check that teams have their Phase A brief, Stage 1 declaration and first Venture Value Map.

Give every stakeholder role the same version of the venture brief and permitted evidence. Prepare five independent responses using the [simulation kit](../03_simulation_kit/simulation_design.md). Review factual support, role boundaries and tone before releasing them. Have a facilitator-reviewed text version available. Never present deterministic demonstration output as a live model response. The prototype's LLM mode is a stub; live integration requires separate confirmation.

Display the five capability clusters, the four decision labels and the final submission requirements. Provide the response log in an accessible editable format. Keep human participant records outside this repository. Confirm locally before use: exact venue, team capacity, tool configuration and submission route.

## Simulation: 90 minutes

| Elapsed minute | Facilitator action and suggested wording | Team action or checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Welcome teams. State that this voluntary Challenge is separate from module assessment. Explain the session sequence. | Locate materials and request any immediate access support. |
| 2 to 5 | Say: “The AI feedback is provisional and non-authoritative. It represents a stakeholder position and may contain errors. You are responsible for checking claims and deciding what to do. You may adopt, partially adopt, reject or defer a suggestion, with reasons. The Regulator role gives no legal advice.” | Identify who will maintain the response log and who will track assumptions. |
| 5 to 8 | Explain the evidence rule: “All roles use the same supplied facts. An unverified statement stays an assumption. Flag anything presented as fact without support.” | Locate sources and label untested assumptions. |
| 8 to 10 | Explain assessment: “We assess the quality of your reasoning. Agreeing with AI and producing a polished presentation earn no credit by themselves.” Confirm the five clusters. | Check rubric access and clarify task boundaries. |
| 10 to 15 | Ask teams to freeze the baseline brief and first map for this session. Check that feedback will address that version. | State the opportunity, revenue logic, main uncertainty and current recommendation in working notes. |
| 15 to 20 | Introduce the five roles and any declared variants. Complete the final human review of prepared responses; hold unsafe output. | Read the response-log headings and identify the distinction between stakeholder interests and factual claims. |
| 20 to 25 | Release Investor feedback, or its lender / green-finance variant. Ask: “Which assumption about viability or risk is being challenged?” | Summarise the challenge and note the evidence requested. |
| 25 to 30 | Release Customer feedback, or its enterprise-buyer variant. Ask: “What does this customer need to trust or adopt the offer?” | Record the challenge and any tension with the first role. |
| 30 to 35 | Release Supplier / Operations Partner feedback. Ask: “Which promise depends on delivery capacity or another party?” | Record deliverability concerns and dependencies. |
| 35 to 40 | Release Regulator feedback. Repeat: “This is a simulated concern requiring verification. It cannot establish permission or compliance.” | Record procedural or safety questions and where authoritative confirmation would be needed. |
| 40 to 45 | Release Community / Ethics Stakeholder feedback. Ask: “Who gains, who bears risk, and which mechanism would address the harm?” | Record access, distributional or sustainability concerns. |
| 45 to 50 | Take clarification questions. Correct unsupported statements visibly and distribute corrections to affected teams. Preserve legitimate priority conflicts. | Separate factual errors from disagreements about value or acceptable risk. |
| 50 to 55 | Provide a short break from panel discussion and quiet reading time. Keep materials accessible. | Review the five challenge summaries individually before team discussion. |
| 55 to 60 | Say: “Identify the central conflict. State why these stakeholders matter for your venture now. Agreement with every role is not required.” | Prioritise the main conflict and identify alternatives. |
| 60 to 70 | Prompt every decision row: “What is your decision, why, and what evidence would change it?” Ask teams to specify the retained and changed parts of partial adoption. | Complete `ADOPT`, `PARTIALLY ADOPT`, `REJECT` or `DEFER` decisions with reasons and evidence conditions. |
| 70 to 80 | Ask teams to connect decisions to concrete revision. Prompt review of exposure, readiness gaps and anticipated harms in the value map. | Draft changes to the venture and map; record a provisional proceed, pivot, pause or stop judgement. |
| 80 to 85 | Ask teams to check consistency across brief, log and map. Say: “Keep unresolved questions visible. A proposed test must remain labelled as a proposal.” | Check evidence labels and identify the memo's central recommendation. |
| 85 to 88 | Give a three-minute completion prompt. Confirm the Phase C deadline and defence arrangements, date to be confirmed. | Submit the response log through the approved route and retain the baseline. |
| 88 to 90 | Close: “Your next task is to defend your choices with evidence, including what you rejected or deferred and why. The panel's feedback remains provisional.” | Note remaining revisions and the final pack requirements. |

If unsafe content appears, pause delivery, withdraw it and issue a corrected version. If a disruption changes students' opportunity to reason, document it and provide equivalent time or reschedule. Record the actual duration; do not claim adherence to this 90-minute schedule after an overrun. Use the reviewed text route if the live system cannot provide suitable feedback.

## Presentation and oral defence: proposed 20-minute team slot

This default allocates three minutes to the presentation, ten minutes to the structured oral defence and time for opening, close, scoring and turnover. The defence itself remains within the 8 to 12 minutes set in the playbook. Choose an agreed protocol before the event; incorporate individual adjustments without treating them as weaker performance. Team slots do not certify each student's individual attainment.

| Elapsed minute | Facilitator or judge action | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | Confirm the team pack and agreed adjustments. Say: “Explain your team's reasoning in your own words. AI feedback remains provisional. You can acknowledge uncertainty. During questions, answer without live generative-AI assistance; agreed assistive support remains available.” | Team readiness and shared understanding of the process. |
| 1 to 4 | Invite the presentation. Give a visible one-minute remaining signal at minute 3. Ask for the recommendation and the principal change after feedback. | A clear, evidence-linked venture argument. |
| 4 to 6 | Opportunity Recognition: “Which observation best supports this opportunity, and how did your framing change?” Use a brief follow-up if needed. | Credible observation and evolution of framing. |
| 6 to 8 | Feasibility Judgement: “Why proceed, pivot, pause or stop? Which assumption could change that decision?” | Evidence for and against, alternatives and uncertainty. |
| 8 to 10 | Stakeholder Reasoning: “Which stakeholder conflict mattered most? Defend one decision to reject, defer or only partly adopt feedback.” If all feedback was adopted, ask which adoption was most contestable. | Prioritisation and a reasoned feedback decision. |
| 10 to 12 | Ethical Positioning: “What harm or trade-off remains, and what specific mechanism addresses it?” Refer to the map where helpful. | Consequences, responsibility and mechanism credibility. |
| 12 to 14 | Venture Communication: “Explain your final recommendation to the stakeholder most likely to challenge it. What should they understand about the remaining uncertainty?” | Audience-aware explanation and independent command of the argument. |
| 14 to 15 | Close the questions. Explain how section 7, Oral Defence Excerpt, will be completed from actual questions and answers and checked for accuracy. | An accurate excerpt without reconstructed success claims. |
| 15 to 18 | Judges record independent cluster judgements with evidence references. Team leaves the assessment space. | Rubric-based reasons, including unresolved evidence gaps. |
| 18 to 20 | Judges flag differences for moderation and prepare the next slot. Keep participant notes in the approved location. | Clear separation of observation, interpretation and score rationale. |

Use the [oral defence guide](../02_assessment_kit/oral_defence_guide.md) for equivalent questions and the [judge calibration guide](../02_assessment_kit/judge_calibration_guide.md) for three-assessor preparation. Allow thinking time, avoid rapid-fire interrogation and invite contributions across members. Judge the evidence in the full pack alongside oral responses. Fluent speech, confidence and accent are not substitutes for reasoning.

## After the sessions

Check the final pack for the revised venture, one-page memo, revised map, final declaration and actual defence excerpt. Keep personal data and live submissions outside the repository. Collect participant, judge and observer feedback only through the approved arrangements and instruments. Record actual delivery conditions, corrections and accessibility adjustments in the authorised location without unnecessary personal detail. Do not label voluntary feedback as completed before it is received.

This run sheet separates the simulation from the defence to protect synthesis time, and it uses the canonical five capability clusters and four decision labels.
