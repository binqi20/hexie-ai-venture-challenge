# Judge calibration guide

Version 1.0, October 2026. This is a protocol for three human assessors before the early December run. No human calibration session or agreement result is claimed here. AI-assisted synthetic checks are internal QA and do not replace this session.

## Shared anchors

| Level | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Advanced | Exercises independent judgement, uses evidence selectively and credibly, makes assumptions explicit, considers alternatives, and can defend pivots under challenge. |
| Proficient | Sound reasoning and sufficient evidence, but some assumptions, alternatives, or implications remain underdeveloped. |
| Developing | Partial understanding and some relevant work, but reasoning is incomplete, weakly integrated, or dependent on generic claims. |
| Emerging | Cannot yet demonstrate the capability reliably; claims are unsupported, confused, or not defensible when questioned. |

Use these anchors with the unchanged cluster descriptors and default weights in `capability_rubric.md`. Record five independent cluster scores before calculating the total. The scored unit for this Challenge is the team case; do not infer individual mastery from the team result.

## What assessors should resist

Charisma, confident delivery, visual polish, fluent AI-assisted prose, and ESG terminology can draw attention away from the reasoning. Require an evidence pointer for each score and assess the substantive clusters before Venture Communication. Communication rewards clarity, structure, audience awareness, and command in Q&A. It does not reward theatrical confidence.

Apply the three CSV diagnostic flags independently of scores. Flags are prompts for evidence review, not penalties. A team may reject persuasive stakeholder advice with sound reasons. A recommendation to pause or stop can demonstrate advanced judgement. A declaration of extensive AI use does not lower a score, and a declaration of non-use does not raise one.

## Disciplinary flexibility

Retain common capabilities while allowing relevant forms of evidence. A scientific venture may depend on a technical test and a long development pathway; a creative venture may depend on rights, collaborators, and audience access; a social or public-value venture may depend on implementation partnerships and sustained resources. These are possible evidence contexts, not claims about particular modules. Judge constraints and uncertainty within the venture's context, without requiring immediate sales or a conventional investor pitch. Technical elegance alone cannot establish demand, and commercial immaturity alone cannot establish weak reasoning.

The two short vignettes below isolate judging traps. They are discussion aids and do not constitute complete cases or replace the eight-case blind exercise. Their interpretations apply only to the stated evidence.

### SYNTHETIC vignette 1: Refill Loop
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

A fictional team presents an elegant reusable-packaging concept and calls it “responsible by design”. The team estimates that customers will pay more but supplies no test. Its log adopts the Customer's lower-price demand and the Supplier / Operations Partner's more costly collection requirement without explaining how both can hold. In Q&A, speakers repeat the sustainability claim and cannot identify who would operate or pay for collection.

Calibration focus: the statement of intent is not a designed mechanism. The evidence suggests weak feasibility and stakeholder integration, and thin Ethical Positioning. Do not award Proficient on these clusters because the presentation is attractive. Flag ESG vocabulary without mechanisms and conflicting feedback adopted without resolution. Score Venture Communication from the argument and answers, rather than slide appearance. Obtain the full brief before judging Opportunity Recognition.

### SYNTHETIC vignette 2: Thermal Patch
> SYNTHETIC CASE. Generated for internal validation of the HeXie-AI Venture Challenge resources. Not student work.

A fictional team proposes a reusable temperature indicator. Its case includes labelled simulated bench-test evidence and a clear account of what the test cannot establish. It recommends pausing deployment because reliability under transport conditions is untested. The team compares two validation routes, assigns responsibility for a proposed test, identifies resources as team estimates, and states the result that would permit progress. Its slides are plain; its answers explain why avoiding premature use matters to affected users.

Calibration focus: commercial immaturity and plain slides do not justify a low Feasibility Judgement score. The evidence may support a strong score if the full case addresses its material constraints and alternatives. A test plan can demonstrate reasoning without proving reliability. Examine Ethical Positioning for alternatives and concrete safeguards, and judge communication from the explanation. Do not infer the other cluster scores from technical detail alone.

## Calibration session protocol

1. **Prepare the pack.** The coordinator selects eight complete SYNTHETIC cases, two intended at each level, with varied disciplinary contexts. Use neutral case IDs and remove intended levels, generation prompts, prior scores, and answer keys from all assessor copies. Preserve complete venture evidence, including the defence excerpt. An authorised coordinator holds the intended-level mapping separately. Assessors must not open generation prompts or answer keys. The two vignettes above are excluded from the eight cases.
2. **Freeze the basis.** Record the actual date, three pseudonymous rater codes, case IDs, file versions, rubric version, default weights, and scoring rules. Give every assessor the same pack and a separate copy of the CSV. Confirm that names, consent choices, and irrelevant identifying information are absent. No participant data are needed.
3. **Blind independent scoring.** Before the discussion, each assessor scores all eight cases across five clusters, records evidence and diagnostic flags, and calculates totals and overall levels. No peer scores, AI-generated scores, or intended levels are available during this stage. Reserve approximately 60 to 80 minutes as a planning estimate, adjusting after a dry run. Collect the three completed sheets before opening discussion.
4. **Calculate initial agreement.** Preserve all 24 case-rater rows and 120 cluster scores. For each cluster, count cases where all three raters give the same score, reported out of eight. Across all clusters, report exact pairwise agreement: equal-score pairs divided by 120 (three rater pairs × eight cases × five clusters). Also record each case-cluster's minimum, maximum, and range. Record overall-level agreement separately; matching totals can conceal cluster disagreement. These descriptive measures are not a validation claim.
5. **Discuss disagreements.** Reserve about 60 minutes. Start with ranges of two or more levels, threshold disagreements, and flags. Each assessor cites the evidence and descriptor behind the original score. Review charisma, language, discipline, AI-use, and ESG halo risks. Use the vignettes after initial scoring to discuss the two traps. Only after evidence-led discussion may the coordinator disclose intended levels; intended labels are hypotheses, not a correct-answer authority.
6. **Rescore and record agreement.** Each assessor independently records any revised score and reason in a separate round. Recalculate agreement with the same denominators. Record an agreed interpretation or remaining disagreement for every discussed item. Preserve original scores. If a consensus score is also recorded, label it clearly; consensus after discussion is not independent reliability.
7. **Confirm readiness.** Resolve how all five clusters, diagnostic flags, weightings, and accessibility arrangements will be used. If a two-level disagreement remains, clarify evidence expectations and repeat independent scoring on a suitable additional case before the live run. This is a proposed operational trigger, not a validated reliability threshold. Record residual one-level differences and the live moderation route. The project leader confirms readiness and any follow-up date; do not change canonical descriptors unilaterally.
