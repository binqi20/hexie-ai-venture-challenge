# Oral defence guide

Version 1.0, October 2026. The defence authenticates understanding through a structured, supportive conversation. It is not adversarial. For the December Challenge, the working format is one team defence of 8 to 12 minutes after the presentation. This team-level evidence cannot establish every member's individual attainment and does not change module marks. The project leader should confirm the final team schedule with the playbook owner.

## Format

Publish the rubric, question themes, AI boundaries, and accessibility route in advance. Allow reference to the submitted pack. Answers must express participants' own reasoning; live generative answer production is outside this defence format. Agree assistive communication or translation arrangements before the session so that access support remains available.

| Segment | 8-minute version | 10-minute default | 12-minute version |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Welcome and explanation | 0.5 minutes | 0.5 minutes | 0.5 minutes |
| Five core questions, one per cluster | 5 minutes | 6.5 minutes | 8 minutes |
| Targeted clarification or evidence follow-up | 2 minutes | 2.5 minutes | 3 minutes |
| Final clarification and close | 0.5 minutes | 0.5 minutes | 0.5 minutes |

Use the same standard duration within a round, with agreed accessibility adjustments. Reading and private scoring happen outside this time. A lead questioner manages pace; other assessors listen and record evidence without interrupting. Ask one question per cluster, including Ethical Positioning, before discretionary follow-ups. Select comparable demands across teams and ask one question at a time. Rotate opportunities to answer without assuming equal airtime proves equal contribution. If time or an access problem prevents coverage, arrange an equivalent follow-up before final scoring.

Opening script: “We will ask how you reached your recommendation. You may refer to your pack, ask us to rephrase, and explain what remains uncertain. We are interested in your reasoning, including why you challenged or rejected AI suggestions.”

## Question bank

Choose from each cluster; the bank is not a requirement to ask every question.

### Opportunity Recognition

1. What observation or signal first suggested this opportunity, and how credible is it?
2. How did your framing change between Phase A and your final brief, and why?
3. Which alternative opportunity did you reject, and what made this one more appropriate?
4. Why does the opportunity matter now for this customer or community, and what evidence could undermine that claim?

### Feasibility Judgement

1. Which assumption most strongly determines your proceed / pivot / pause / stop judgement, and how has it been tested?
2. What is the strongest evidence against your recommendation, and how have you accounted for it?
3. If available time or resources were reduced, what would you change and what would remain infeasible?
4. Which technical, regulatory, market, or resource constraint could stop progress, and what evidence would change your decision?
5. Which readiness gap in your Venture Value Map affects feasibility most, and how would its mechanism be funded and delivered?

### Stakeholder Reasoning

1. Whose cooperation matters first, and what dependency makes that priority defensible?
2. Which stakeholder demands conflicted, and how did your response resolve or acknowledge the conflict?
3. Explain one ADOPT, PARTIALLY ADOPT, REJECT, or DEFER decision and the evidence that would change it.
4. Who bears a cost under your recommendation, and how would you engage them before proceeding?

### Ethical Positioning

1. Who could be harmed even if the venture achieves its commercial aims?
2. What was the hardest ethical trade-off, which alternative did you consider, and what value or principle informed the choice?
3. Identify one protective mechanism, its owner and cost, and explain whether it is intended, designed, or tested.
4. Where does social value reinforce or conflict with commercial value, and who pays for your chosen response?
5. Which Regulator or Community / Ethics Stakeholder concern changed your plan, or why was it rejected?

### Venture Communication

1. State your core claim and the strongest supporting evidence in a concise explanation.
2. How would you explain the same recommendation to a technical partner and to a community participant?
3. Which part of your presentation could be misunderstood, and how would you clarify it?
4. What would you change in your explanation after today's questions, and why?

Use the AI declarations for a targeted follow-up, such as: “Show us the claim you checked and explain your verification”, or “Why did you reject that AI suggestion?” Link the answer to its substantive cluster; AI fluency earns no additional marks.

## Scoring principles

Read the pack before the defence and note provisional evidence, not a settled overall impression. Each human assessor then records a whole-number score for each cluster using the canonical descriptors. Link each score to a pack section and/or an answer. An answer may clarify an unclear submission, expose a contradiction, or show a limit; revise the relevant cluster with a reason. Do not replace five cluster judgements with a single impression of performance.

Distinguish limited understanding from limited time, unfamiliar wording, anxiety, or access failure. Give one neutral clarification opportunity when a mismatch appears. A candid explanation that evidence is missing can show uncertainty discipline, though it does not substitute for the missing evidence. Consult the rubric for weights and diagnostic flags. Record disagreement before moderation. Neither automated scoring nor an AI detector determines results.

## Fairness, language, and accessibility

Use plain language without idioms or unnecessary technical traps. Rephrase a question without reducing its conceptual demand. Allow brief thinking time, notes, and requests for repetition. Assess an intelligible argument and responsive reasoning; accent, speech speed, grammatical perfection, confidence, and eye contact are not scoring criteria.

Invite confidential access requests before the event without collecting diagnoses in assessment notes. Offer written display of questions, accessible documents, breaks, and agreed assistive communication. Where speech is a barrier, use a live typed exchange or another agreed interactive format with the same cluster coverage and reasoning demand. Adjust time where required and record only the adjustment, not medical details. Provide an orientation and practice question on a different topic to all teams.

Participants may stop an uncomfortable AI interaction and use the alternative route in the responsible-AI plan. Research non-consent does not restrict participation or recognition. Keep consent choices outside rater materials wherever practicable.

## Record and limits

The Phase C excerpt uses `## 7. Oral Defence Excerpt` with five actual judge questions, one per cluster, and the corresponding team answers. A concise written record is sufficient; label paraphrases and let the team check accuracy. No audio or video recording is required or authorised by this guide. Research use follows consent and data minimisation rules. Do not fabricate an excerpt if no defence occurred.

These format choices should be reviewed after the first live run. They do not demonstrate improved learning.
