# Capability rubric

Version 1.0, October 2026. Design resource for the first live run in early December 2026; no live assessment results are reported here.

## Five capability clusters and default weights

| Cluster | Default weight | What it assesses |
|---|---:|---|
| Opportunity Recognition | 25% | Framing a specific opportunity from credible observation, and showing how the framing evolved |
| Feasibility Judgement | 25% | A disciplined proceed / pivot / pause / stop judgement using explicit assumptions, evidence, alternatives, and uncertainty |
| Stakeholder Reasoning | 20% | Identifying and prioritising stakeholders, explaining tensions among them, and deciding which feedback to act on |
| Ethical Positioning | 15% | Recognising harms, rights, fairness, and sustainability implications, and defending a position with mechanisms rather than slogans |
| Venture Communication | 15% | A clear, audience-aware argument and Q&A answers that show independent command of the reasoning |

Weights are defaults that module leaders may adapt. Polish, charisma, visual design, and AI fluency earn no credit by themselves.

## The rubric: four levels

Shared anchors:

| Level | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Advanced | Exercises independent judgement, uses evidence selectively and credibly, makes assumptions explicit, considers alternatives, and can defend pivots under challenge. |
| Proficient | Sound reasoning and sufficient evidence, but some assumptions, alternatives, or implications remain underdeveloped. |
| Developing | Partial understanding and some relevant work, but reasoning is incomplete, weakly integrated, or dependent on generic claims. |
| Emerging | Cannot yet demonstrate the capability reliably; claims are unsupported, confused, or not defensible when questioned. |

Cluster descriptors:

| Cluster | Advanced | Proficient | Developing | Emerging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opportunity Recognition | Specific, non-obvious opportunity grounded in credible observation or stakeholder signals; explains why now and how the framing changed | Plausible opportunity with some evidence of problem relevance and some refinement | General problem or idea; framing broad, derivative, or weakly evidenced | Loose idea or trend with no clear opportunity logic |
| Feasibility Judgement | Explicit proceed / pivot / pause / stop judgement using stated assumptions, evidence for and against, alternatives, and uncertainty bounds across market, technical, regulatory, and resource dimensions | Coherent view across several dimensions; major assumptions and risks acknowledged | Partial analysis relying on assertion or optimism; assumptions implicit | Guesswork; critical constraints ignored; aspiration confused with evidence |
| Stakeholder Reasoning | Right stakeholders, credibly prioritised; tensions and dependencies explained; feedback adopted, rejected, or deferred with reasons | Most key stakeholders identified with some prioritisation and reasoned decisions | Stakeholders listed with little prioritisation or trade-off reasoning; tends to adopt all feedback or dismiss it without reasons | Stakeholders mentioned superficially, usually only customers and investors |
| Ethical Positioning | Harms and sustainability issues recognised early; alternatives compared; a defended position backed by concrete mechanisms | Relevant issues identified; defensible position with some recognition of trade-offs | Obvious concerns named; analysis thin, reactive, or limited to compliance language or ESG vocabulary | Ethics ignored or treated as a slogan |
| Venture Communication | Clear, well-organised argument with a strong central claim; Q&A answers reveal independent thinking | Coherent story with relevant support; mostly effective Q&A | Understandable but uneven or dense; Q&A shows limited command | Unfocused; answers evasive, memorised, or disconnected |

## Scoring and recording

Code each cluster using whole numbers: Emerging 1, Developing 2, Proficient 3, Advanced 4. Judge the best-supported descriptor from the submission and oral defence, with a short evidence reference for each score. Score the first four clusters before Venture Communication. Use the shared anchors to resolve boundaries; do not score by counting keywords.

Weighted total = (0.25 × Opportunity Recognition) + (0.25 × Feasibility Judgement) + (0.20 × Stakeholder Reasoning) + (0.15 × Ethical Positioning) + (0.15 × Venture Communication).

| Weighted total | Overall level |
|---|---|
| ≥3.50 | Advanced |
| 2.50 to 3.49 | Proficient |
| 1.50 to 2.49 | Developing |
| <1.50 | Emerging |

Totals range from 1.00 to 4.00. Display two decimal places. Apply thresholds to the unrounded total: 3.50 and above, 2.50 to below 3.50, 1.50 to below 2.50, and below 1.50. This also avoids gaps if locally adapted weights produce additional decimal places. Any weight adaptation must be agreed and communicated before scoring, sum to 100%, and be recorded with the scores.

Use `scoring_sheet_template.csv`, one row per case and rater. It is a header-only CSV and does not calculate totals automatically. Record pseudonymous case and rater codes, never student identifiers. Blank means pending or unavailable, never zero. Do not calculate a total until all five clusters have a defensible score. Emerging requires evidence of that level; an inaccessible or omitted assessment opportunity requires follow-up, not an automatic 1.

Use `TRUE` or `FALSE` for each diagnostic flag after review; leave blank until reviewed. The flags identify polish exceeding demonstrated substance, ESG language without mechanisms, and unresolved adoption of incompatible feedback. Explain every TRUE flag in notes with an evidence location. Flags trigger scrutiny of the relevant descriptors and carry no automatic deduction or additional weight. Preserve each rater's scores before moderation and distinguish any later agreed row in the rater code and notes.

## Evidence each cluster may credit

These are evidence options within the seven-component pack, not additional compulsory submissions. A proposed test is evidence of planning; it is not evidence that the test succeeded.

| Cluster | Creditable evidence and where to find it |
|---|---|
| Opportunity Recognition | A specific problem and source of insight; credible observations or stakeholder signals; changes between the first and revised venture; alternatives considered and reasons for rejecting them. Use the venture brief, decision memo, and oral account to trace how the opportunity took shape. |
| Feasibility Judgement | Stated assumptions with tested or untested status; evidence supporting and challenging the venture; resource, implementation, technical, and regulatory constraints where relevant; a justified proceed / pivot / pause / stop recommendation. Use the assumptions table, decision memo, Venture Value Map, and defence. |
| Stakeholder Reasoning | Who gains or bears costs; who can enable or block progress; dependencies and a reasoned order of engagement. Credit prioritisation and explanations for adopting, partially adopting, rejecting, or deferring feedback in the response log and defence. |
| Ethical Positioning | Identified harms, rights, fairness and sustainability implications; a stated ethical basis for a choice; comparison with a rejected alternative; practical mitigation and its limits. Credit the decision memo, anticipated harms, mechanisms in the Venture Value Map, and defended answers. |
| Venture Communication | An intelligible central claim, a logical sequence, relevant support, and answers responsive to the question and audience. Use the venture brief, presentation, and defence together. An answer that explains uncertainty can demonstrate stronger command than an unsupported confident assertion. |

AI declarations help locate verification and human decisions. A declaration alone does not establish capability, and AI-generated stakeholder statements are not observations of actual customers, regulators, or investors.

## How the Venture Value Lens informs scoring

The Venture Value Map supplies evidence to three existing clusters. It adds no sixth cluster, separate ESG score, or letter rating. The same entry may support different clusters only where the rater explains the distinct reasoning demonstrated.

| Cluster | How to interpret the map |
|---|---|
| Ethical Positioning | Examine whether material topics identify plausible harms and affected rights, whether alternatives were compared, and whether the response contains a specific mechanism with ownership and costs. Exposure and readiness labels alone earn no credit. |
| Stakeholder Reasoning | Examine whose interests reinforce or conflict, who pays for a trade-off, how dependencies affect priorities, and whether the response addresses Regulator and Community / Ethics Stakeholder challenges. Agreement with every persona does not demonstrate resolution. |
| Feasibility Judgement | Examine whether mitigation costs, readiness gaps, implementation capacity, and time horizons change the recommendation. A high-exposure topic needs readiness of at least 2; medium exposure needs at least 1; low exposure has no minimum. A shortfall is a gap to reason about, not an automatic failure. |

Readiness means 0 None; 1 Stated intent; 2 Designed mechanism (specific, owned, costed); 3 Tested mechanism (pilot or evidence). Claiming readiness 3 requires supporting evidence. Teams may demonstrate strong judgement by identifying a gap and defending pause or stop. The relationship categories are Reinforcing, Neutral, Trade-off, and Hidden risk. Score the reasoning behind them.

Use the project interpretation in the [Venture Value Lens method](../04_venture_value_lens/value_lens_method.md). Its ESG-derived logic is contextual evidence, not proof of venture sustainability or operating performance.

## Anti-halo rules

- Give no credit for polish, charisma, visual design, AI fluency, or ESG vocabulary without mechanisms and defensible reasoning.
- Require a concrete evidence pointer for every cluster. Do not carry an impressive communication score into another cluster.
- A strong technical prototype, existing sales, or familiar business model cannot replace explanation. Venture maturity and access to contacts are not capabilities in this rubric.
- Trace conflicting feedback to the response log. Adopting incompatible demands without a credible resolution should be considered against Stakeholder Reasoning and, where relevant, Feasibility Judgement.
- Judge clarity and command, without rewarding accent, speed, confidence, or stylistic conformity. Use the accessibility arrangements in the oral defence guide.
- Treat legitimate AI use and non-use equally. Resolve incomplete declarations through proportionate clarification; do not infer misconduct from polished prose or use detector outputs as proof.
