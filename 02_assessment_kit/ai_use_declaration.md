**Staff note: proportionate disclosure**

These short declarations make the contribution of AI and human judgement discussable. Require both checkpoints, including a brief no-use statement where applicable. Focus on substantive support for ideas, evidence interpretation, alternatives, or drafting. Routine spelling, grammar, and formatting assistance may be noted briefly; do not request every prompt, private account history, or screenshots. A short account of a consequential check or rejection is more useful than a tool inventory. No additional evidence appendix is required by this template.

Read the declaration alongside the venture brief, decision memo, and response log. In the defence, ask students to explain one declared change, how a factual claim was checked, or why feedback was rejected. Use a follow-up to distinguish a tested claim from an assumption. Assess the resulting reasoning within the relevant capability cluster. Disclosure earns no separate score, and legitimate AI use or non-use attracts no penalty. Seek clarification if statements conflict; a polished submission or sparse declaration alone does not establish misconduct.

XJTLU's *Guidelines on the Use of AI in Research Funding Applications* distinguish low-impact support from substantive collaboration and retain human accountability. This template adapts those principles for the Challenge; those guidelines do not establish a student module assessment policy. Research reuse of declarations requires appropriate consent under your institution's research-ethics requirements. Never request personal, sensitive, confidential, or identifiable content to demonstrate transparency.
