# Adaptation guide

Version 1.0, October 2026

## Start with the learning decision

Choose whether to run the existing challenge, adapt its context, or reuse the design pattern in another discipline. State which student decisions the activity should make visible and which evidence would demonstrate the relevant capabilities. Do not assume that a fluent pitch demonstrates understanding or that disciplinary sophistication resolves stakeholder tensions.

The initial December run is voluntary and separate from module assessment. Any later assessed use needs local assessment review. The project ethics approval does not automatically cover another research setting or a changed protocol.

## Preserve the core

| Stable element | What must remain visible |
|---|---|
| Capability logic | Opportunity Recognition, Feasibility Judgement, Stakeholder Reasoning, Ethical Positioning and Venture Communication; evidence-led four-level descriptors |
| Explicit AI-use expectations | Advance explanation of permitted use and responsibility; Stage 1 and final declarations showing verification, revision and rejection |
| Stakeholder conflict | Shared facts with different priorities, risk tolerances and time horizons; a meaningful decision that cannot satisfy every preference simultaneously |
| Student judgement and defence | A response log, reasons for adoption or rejection, evidence that would change decisions, and structured oral defence |
| Human oversight | A facilitator reviews feedback; human assessors make judgements; staff can pause, replace or withdraw inappropriate output |
| Venture Value Lens | All nine steps below; commercial and social value examined together; no sixth assessment cluster or venture letter rating |

Preserve the distinction between evidence, a team assumption and a proposed test. Keep all fictional exemplars labelled SYNTHETIC. Personas represent roles and interests; do not introduce demographic stereotypes or real people.

## Keep the Value Lens sequence

1. **Classify:** choose the closest SASB SICS industry and, if useful, a secondary industry. Explain the fit to the venture's activity.
2. **Select material topics:** choose 3 to 5 relevant disclosure topics; optionally add one venture-specific topic with a reason.
3. **Rate exposure:** use Low, Medium or High, explaining the business-model, customer, supply-chain or location basis.
4. **Rate management readiness:** 0 None; 1 Stated intent; 2 Designed mechanism, specific, owned and costed; 3 Tested mechanism, with pilot or other evidence.
5. **Apply exposure-scaled expectations:** High exposure requires readiness of at least 2; Medium requires at least 1; Low has no minimum. Flag shortfalls.
6. **Classify the commercial and social value relationship:** Reinforcing, Neutral, Trade-off or Hidden risk. Explain who pays where value conflicts.
7. **Weight topics:** High, Medium or Low by impact and time horizon, with reasons.
8. **Anticipate harms:** address challenges raised by the Regulator and Community / Ethics Stakeholder and document the response.
9. **Write the alignment thesis:** explain where commercial and social value reinforce or conflict and what the venture will do.

Use `04_venture_value_lens/value_lens_method.md` for the method and [REFERENCES.md](../REFERENCES.md) for citations. The method adapts industry materiality and exposure/readiness logic from SASB, MSCI and FTSE Russell. It does not transfer peer-relative normalisation, controversy deductions, governance deduction models, audited performance metrics or public-data-only rules. Do not reproduce third-party tables or imply provider endorsement.

## Adapt the local details

| Adaptable element | Practical change | Check before use |
|---|---|---|
| Venture context and examples | Replace the problem, customer, resource constraints and evidence forms | The task still requires a consequential decision; assumptions remain labelled |
| Personas | Adjust role priorities or use the lender / green-finance and enterprise-buyer variants | Preserve credible conflicting interests and shared facts; retain scrutiny of harms and public interests |
| Timing | Spread preparation and revision across sessions; adjust within local constraints | Retain a complete decision-and-defence cycle; reconcile participant burden with consent |
| Mode | Onsite, online or reviewed text/role-card delivery | Equivalent access to feedback and opportunities to explain reasoning; approved data handling |
| Weights | Change default weights where outcomes justify it | Explain and publish the change before activity; keep the five clusters and recalibrate |
| Disciplinary vocabulary | Use local terms for evidence, feasibility and value | Keep the underlying constructs recognisable; explain unfamiliar terms |
| SASB industry | Select a different primary or secondary industry | Match the venture's operations and exposures, rather than treating a module code as an industry |

For a substantial disciplinary redesign, map each local outcome to the unchanged capability constructs. If a capability cannot be meaningfully assessed, document the mismatch and seek design review before presenting the result as the same Challenge. The simulation-specific guidance is `03_simulation_kit/adaptation_guide.md`.

## Suggested starting points for the ten EEM modules

All entries below are **suggestions**, drawn from the project's context-to-industry mapping in `04_venture_value_lens/industry_topics.md`. Persona emphases are proposed adaptations. They do not establish module content, leader agreement, deployment or a compulsory industry classification. Review the suggested industry and its topics against the actual venture.

| Module | Suggested SASB industry and code | Suggested persona emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| APH114 | Biotechnology & Pharmaceuticals (HC-BP) | Regulator and Customer: safety, credible evidence and access |
| DES107 | Building Products & Furnishings (CG-BF) | Supplier / Operations Partner and Community / Ethics Stakeholder: materials, lifecycle and deliverability |
| FTA123 | Media & Entertainment (SV-ME) | Customer and Community / Ethics Stakeholder: audience trust, rights and representation |
| HSS102 | Education (SV-ED) | Community / Ethics Stakeholder and Customer: access, service quality and claims about benefit |
| INF101TC | Software & IT Services (TC-SI) | Regulator and enterprise-buyer variant: data handling, reliability and evidence of delivery capability |
| MTH128 | Solar Technology & Project Developers (RR-ST) | Supplier / Operations Partner and Regulator: technical feasibility, infrastructure and environmental constraints |
| SAT102 | Software & IT Services (TC-SI) | Investor and Supplier / Operations Partner: technical uncertainty, scaling and operational dependence |
| SCI102 | Agricultural Products (FB-AG) | Supplier / Operations Partner and Community / Ethics Stakeholder: sourcing, resource use and safety |
| SMO106 | E-Commerce (CG-EC) | Customer and enterprise-buyer variant: problem fit, service reliability and distribution |
| SMO108 | E-Commerce (CG-EC) | Lender / green-finance variant and Community / Ethics Stakeholder: viability, environmental claims and supporting evidence |

An industry suggestion is a starting hypothesis. When none fits, compare the activities generating revenue, costs and exposure; document the closest fit and its limits. A secondary industry can capture a material activity missed by the primary classification.

Before launch, trial one complete SYNTHETIC case through the adapted brief, feedback, response log, Value Map and defence. Check that staff can apply every cluster with evidence. Ask a colleague to challenge the main stakeholder tension and exposure/readiness judgements. Confirm locally before use: permissions, staff capacity and the suitability of any chosen adaptation, through the adopting module leader's review and, where relevant, institutional ethics or assessment decisions. No adoption or educational effect is claimed by this guide.
