# Making entrepreneurial judgement visible in AI-supported assessment

HeXie-AI Venture Challenge | EASE-R1-2526-85 | Version 1.0, October 2026

## Context and problem

AI can help a team produce a plausible venture proposal without making its own reasoning visible. This design addresses that assessment problem by requiring students to explain evidence, resolve stakeholder tensions and defend revisions. Its intended context is entrepreneurship education across the ten EEM modules listed in `01_playbook/competition_playbook.md`. The first live run is scheduled for early December 2026 at IBSS, with volunteers from SMO106, SMO108 and other IBSS undergraduate and postgraduate cohorts. Participation will be voluntary and separate from module assessment. No students, judges or observers have taken part in the Challenge yet.

## Innovation and design rationale

The design combines capability-based assessment with a moderated stakeholder panel. Five roles work from shared facts while applying different priorities. Students decide which feedback to adopt, partially adopt, reject or defer, stating reasons and evidence that would change their decision. Oral defence gives human assessors an opportunity to probe the reasoning behind a polished submission.

The Venture Value Lens adds a structured examination of commercial and social value. Teams select material topics, assess exposure and management readiness, identify gaps, explain value relationships and anticipate harms. The Lens supplies evidence for existing capabilities. It does not create another rubric cluster or issue an ESG letter rating. Its adaptations of SASB, MSCI and FTSE Russell logic are documented in `04_venture_value_lens/value_lens_method.md`, with sources in [REFERENCES.md](../REFERENCES.md).

The project's interpretation of HeXie connects structured milestones, explicit AI boundaries and capability criteria with room for entrepreneurial initiative under conflicting feedback. This is a design interpretation, developed in `00_design/hexie_design_logic.md`. The proposed mechanism is that a visible record of assumptions, alternatives and defended choices may help assessors distinguish independent judgement from output fluency. That proposition has not yet been tested with students.

## How it works

Phase A produces a venture brief, Stage 1 AI-use declaration and first Venture Value Map. In Phase B, the moderated panel challenges the venture from Investor, Customer, Supplier / Operations Partner, Regulator, and Community / Ethics Stakeholder perspectives. Teams complete a response log. In Phase C, they revise the venture and Value Map, write a decision memo and final declaration, then present and undertake an 8 to 12 minute structured oral defence.

Human assessors apply Opportunity Recognition, Feasibility Judgement, Stakeholder Reasoning, Ethical Positioning and Venture Communication. Credit follows evidence and reasoning. Charisma, presentation polish and AI fluency earn no credit by themselves. Facilitators review feedback, remove unsupported claims and provide accessible alternatives. The operating principle is “Human-led pedagogy, AI-assisted simulation, no AI-only decisions about students”.

## Evidence to date

The project's consultation record supports specific design inputs. An accounting academic, a current XJTLU Economics & Finance student and an alumnus informed attention to evidence and responsible AI use. A STEM academic and an innovation-and-strategy PhD candidate informed technical-feasibility and disciplinary adaptation questions. A green-finance manager at a commercial bank informed lender scrutiny and the Value Lens. A planning manager at a large trading enterprise and a green-finance analyst informed the enterprise-buyer variant. Engagement with an external entrepreneurship researcher is recorded, but its discussion focus and design input are not documented. These are consultations, not Challenge participation or systematic student evaluation.

The project's internal evaluation comprised an AI-assisted blind rubric check on 24 SYNTHETIC cases and two rounds of simulation QA. Four of these cases are published as worked examples in `05_evaluation/exemplar_cases/`. These are internal checks.

The blind rubric check matched designed levels in 20/24 cases (83.33%), with all 24 within one level; only 2/24 cases met the readiness rule for every assessed High-exposure topic. Simulation QA found 4 PASS and 4 HOLD in Round 1, followed by 6 PASS and 0 HOLD across four repairs and two regression cases after the binding-constraint prompt revision.

The prototype's 21 automated tests pass, and fresh deterministic exports match the saved demonstration. This is software verification; it does not establish human usability. The Streamlit interface has not been interactively tested, and the LLM interface remains a stub. Internal checks can reveal inconsistencies in resources or software, but cannot establish student learning or judge usability.

## Limits, transferability and next steps

No student evaluation data will be available until the December run. Staff workload, accessibility in use, participant experience, human judge agreement and cross-disciplinary transfer remain unknown. The project leader has distinguished approximately 60 minutes of research-only tasks from the 90-minute simulation and separate defence. Issued participant documents must reflect that distinction before recruitment.

Adopters can change the venture, persona emphasis, timing, mode, weights, examples and SASB industry while retaining the capability logic, AI declarations, stakeholder conflict, student defence, human oversight and Value Lens steps. Start with one local trial and record what needed adjustment. Wider adoption should follow evidence from December and two or three contrasting EEM modules.

A January 2027 evaluation addendum is planned. No dissemination presentation has taken place; a spring 2027 Learning and Teaching Colloquium or equivalent EDU-led session is the target. Contact: Dr Binqi Tang, Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University.
