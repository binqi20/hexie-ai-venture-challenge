# Quick start: understand the Challenge in 10 to 15 minutes

Version 1.0, October 2026

## What it is

Teams develop a venture, receive conflicting feedback from five stakeholder roles, revise their proposal, and defend their decisions. AI can support development and simulation. Students remain responsible for checking claims and explaining what they accepted, changed or rejected. Human assessors judge five capabilities. Polished slides, charisma and AI fluency earn no credit by themselves.

This is a prototype and design package. The first live run is scheduled for early December 2026; no Challenge participants or human judges have yet tested it. SYNTHETIC examples are internal practice material.

## Prepare for delivery

Choose a scenario from `03_simulation_kit/scenarios.yaml` and review the five roles in `03_simulation_kit/personas.yaml`: Investor, Customer, Supplier / Operations Partner, Regulator, and Community / Ethics Stakeholder. Use the lender / green-finance or enterprise-buyer variant where relevant. Freeze a common venture brief and assumption set. Roles may disagree about priorities and acceptable risk; they must not invent different facts.

Provide the rubric, submission headings, explicit AI-use expectations and an accessible alternative participation route in advance. Confirm facilitator and assessor availability, safe handling of inputs, submission arrangements and consent. Calibrate three human assessors using `02_assessment_kit/judge_calibration_guide.md` before the December run.

For an offline demonstration, follow `03_simulation_kit/prototype/README.md`. The prototype composes authored feedback deterministically; LLM mode is not connected. Use reviewed text or role cards if the interface is unsuitable. Never enter identifiable or confidential participant material into the demonstration exports.

## Session flow

| Phase | Student work | Staff action |
|---|---|---|
| A: Venture development, before the simulation | Prepare the venture brief, Stage 1 AI-use declaration and first Venture Value Map | Check that assumptions and evidence are distinguishable; clarify permitted AI use |
| B: Stakeholder simulation, 90 minutes | Consider the five perspectives; record challenges and decisions | Follow `01_playbook/facilitator_run_sheet.md`; moderate feedback before release and pause unsupported or inappropriate claims |
| C: Revision and defence, separately scheduled | Revise the venture and Value Map; submit a decision memo and final declaration; present and defend decisions | Use `02_assessment_kit/oral_defence_guide.md`; allow 8 to 12 minutes for structured oral defence, separate from presentation and scoring time |

Suggested opening: “The panel offers perspectives for you to evaluate. Identify the main conflict, decide what to do, and explain the evidence that would change your decision. You remain responsible for your claims.”

In the original project, the approved participant information sheet estimates approximately 60 minutes of research-only tasks; the 90-minute simulation and separate defence are learning activities. Confirm that issued participant documents describe both time commitments before recruitment.

## What students submit

Keep these canonical headings. Combine the relevant templates into one pack; retain the Phase A version to make revision visible.

| Heading | Required content |
|---|---|
| 1. Venture Brief | Up to 400 words: problem, target customer, solution, business model; assumptions table showing tested/untested status and how to test |
| 2. AI-Use Declaration: Stage 1 | Up to 200 words: tools, purposes, output, verification, changes or rejection |
| 3. Stakeholder Response Log | 3.1 Stakeholder challenges received, in the five-role order; 3.2 Team decisions, with stakeholder, key point, decision, reason and evidence that would change it |
| 4. Decision Memo | Up to 500 words: opportunity, evidence, feasibility judgement, conflict, ethical trade-off, recommendation and uncertainty |
| 5. Venture Value Map | 5.1 Industry mapping; 5.2 Material topics; 5.3 Anticipated harms and response; 5.4 Alignment thesis, up to 120 words |
| 6. AI-Use Declaration: Final | Up to 150 words, including where human judgement is most visible and the responsibility statement |
| 7. Oral Defence Excerpt | Five judge questions, one per cluster, with team answers; use the agreed consent-compatible note route |

Decision labels are `ADOPT`, `PARTIALLY ADOPT`, `REJECT` and `DEFER`. The feasibility recommendation is a separate judgement: proceed, pivot, pause or stop. Rejection of feedback and a decision to stop can both demonstrate strong reasoning.

Student templates are in `02_assessment_kit/ai_use_declaration.md`, `02_assessment_kit/stakeholder_response_log_template.md` and `02_assessment_kit/decision_memo_template.md`. Value Map materials are `04_venture_value_lens/materiality_worksheet.md` and `04_venture_value_lens/value_alignment_map_template.md`.

## How to judge

Use Opportunity Recognition (25%), Feasibility Judgement (25%), Stakeholder Reasoning (20%), Ethical Positioning (15%) and Venture Communication (15%). Apply Advanced, Proficient, Developing and Emerging descriptors from the rubric. Record evidence for each cluster before considering an overall result. Ask students to explain a key assumption, a rejected alternative and a defended revision. Use consistent question coverage and agreed accessibility adjustments.

The Value Lens supports Ethical Positioning, Stakeholder Reasoning and Feasibility Judgement. It does not generate a venture rating or automatic marks. Judge the mechanism and evidence behind a readiness claim. A team score does not establish individual mastery. No AI-only decisions about students are permitted.

## Common problems and immediate responses

| Problem | Response |
|---|---|
| The team accepts incompatible advice | Ask which stakeholder priority prevails, who bears the cost, and what evidence could reverse that choice |
| AI text contains an unsupported claim | Pause it, distinguish evidence from assumption, and replace or remove the claim before release |
| The declaration lists tools without explaining judgement | Ask what was checked, changed or rejected; use the final declaration and defence to clarify responsibility |
| Simulation time runs short | Reduce repeated feedback, preserve time for decisions, and record any changed schedule; do not quietly remove defence |
| Participation or access is uneven | Use agreed written materials, reviewed role cards and equivalent defence arrangements; assess reasoning without penalising the adjustment |
| The interface fails | Continue with the reviewed text route; do not introduce an unapproved provider during the session |

## Adapt and seek support

Change venture context, examples and persona emphasis first. If altering timing, mode or weights, record the reason and check outcome alignment. Preserve the capability logic, explicit AI expectations, stakeholder conflict, student defence, human oversight and Value Lens steps. Follow `07_adoption_kit/adaptation_guide.md`.

Confirm locally before use: staffing, teaching preparation time and deployment arrangements, through a dry run and a confirmed delivery record.
